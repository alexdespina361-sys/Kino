# How the cat's clips were checked, and what the checks found

Two kinds of check, and they answer different questions. **The numbers** (`scripts/cat/anim/qa.mts`) say whether a clip is fit to be shown at all: the body holds together, the cat stands on the floor, no bone jumps. **Blind eyes** say whether a clip is any good: a reviewer who is told nothing about it but its length (and whether it loops) sees twelve numbered pictures and says what it is, what is wrong, and marks it. The numbers never said a clip was good; the blind reviews never said a clip was safe. Both were needed, and when they disagreed the pictures won.

## The blind review

- **Who.** Fresh model subagents (Sonnet in the first rounds, Opus from round 3), one per two or three sheets, with no knowledge of the project. Their reports are data: they are quoted here, not obeyed.
- **What they see.** A contact sheet of 12 numbered pictures through the clip (`glsheet.mjs clip --blind=1`), the sheet under a random id, the key (id to clip and variant) kept outside the folder they may open. They are told the clip's length and whether it loops, and the tool's limits: each picture is rendered on its own (so the eyes, ears and tail start afresh in every one), the camera follows the cat (travel is not shown), the thin flaps in the coat are the model's, a tail may be cropped by the edge, a dark panel may be scenery.
- **What they answer.** What the clip is and how sure they are; what is wrong with the body; what is wrong with the motion; marks from 1 to 10 for readability, anatomy and naturalness (10 is a real cat or a studio's work, 5 is a game animation with visible flaws).
- **How it is read.** One judge's mark moves by about a mark from judge to judge, so a mean over a round is read to the nearest half mark and a change smaller than that is noise. From round 11 on, variants are judged as **A/B pairs** (the same clip made two ways, shown in random order, "which is more natural, and how sure are you"); pairs make a judge more lenient (naturalness about 4.7 to 4.9 in pairs against 3.5 when the same kind of clip is judged alone), so a pair says which is better, not how good either is.
- **What it cannot see.** Twelve stills cannot show breath, the lag of a tail, or whether a paw skates. The numbers cover the skating (`gait.mts`: paws slide at most 0.012 of a body length in the walk and the run); breath and lag are looked at in a recording played at speed (`.kino-cat/preview.ps1`, an ffmpeg GIF), by me, not blind.

## The numbers (`qa.mts`)

See the table in the [README](README.md#the-numbers-qamts). Every clip in the shipped file passes the skin numbers (stretch99 at or under about 3.2, flips at or under 0.03, volume between about 0.84 and 1.2, nothing under the floor) except where stated: the climbs carry the highest `hazard` (a share of the mesh blended between two bones turned nearly a half turn apart; the climb's is 0.036) because a cat hanging by its forepaws over a corner winds its limbs as far as the skin can go.

Two tools found what the numbers and the judges did not: `climb-probe` (how far the body and the legs are inside the ledge, in body lengths) found that the first climb had its whole front 0.2 inside the wall (a tube stuck to the corner, no forelegs on the lip, which every judge of round 10 named without being able to say why); the redesigned climb is at 0.083 for the body and 0.046 for the legs. `paw-drift` and `lag` (how far each paw's sole moves in a clip, and how many seconds each part of the body takes to get half way) found that spring follow-through made the tails of short clips late and moved the paws of clips laid on a pose.

### Round 12, what the losses were

- **sit** (round 10 won, 65 %; marks 7/5/5 against 7/4/4): the spring follow-through changed the way the frames before the last were solved, and the viewer's hind leg came to the same fold as the far one, a stub over a stub. A leg is solved from the way it came (the same final posture can end in different knee and hock folds), so the sit now says where the near knee is (`elbow` in `sitSpec`) and the leg lies along the floor.
- **climb** (round 10 won, 60 %; marks 6/5/5 against 7/5/4): the new climb read better (7 against 6, the forepaws on the lip, the belly to the wall) but "moves like a hold and then shoves forward about 35 pixels". The measurement agreed: the hips' speed went to 2.0 to 2.6 body lengths a second in four frames with accelerations of 15 to 22. One key between the pull and the hind legs being over was in the wrong place (0.2 behind the line between its neighbours); moved, the peak speed is 1.8 and the accelerations about half. The engine's marks (`CLIMB_GRAB`, `CLIMB_PULL`, `CLIMB_OVER`) are fixed, so the haul could only be spread inside them.
- **crouch** (round 10 won, 55 %, both 6/5/5): the tail came down in lockstep with the hips (the follow-through's lead plus windows that had been made wide to make up for the lag). The windows were re-measured (the tail got half way at 0.52 s against 0.37 s for the hips).
- **curl** and **peek** were judged last: curl equal (70 %), peek round 12 (70 %, slower, a faint breath, a smaller pop where it wraps).
- Repeated by the judges of curl and peek in both versions: one-picture jumps of the tail tip (13 to 15 pixels), no staggered tuck of the hind legs, no hip roll, a loop that does not flow from its end into its start, no shoulder blades or elbows (the rig).

### Round 13, what was decided

Judged as pairs unless it says "alone"; a call under 65 % is noise, and is written as such.

- **climb**: the heave smoothed (`n3`) beat round 10's (70 %: "a forepaw reads as hooked on the corner, the head ducks and the back rounds as the chest comes over"); the hind foot then told a place a leg can bend to (`n4`, 0.09 of a body length short of the lip, 0.27 under the hips) against `n3`: 55 %, "nearly indistinguishable", the dangling leg a little more flexed. Kept `n4` (not worse, no extra risk). Both judges named the same last fault: the first ~0.4 s is a dead hang that should sag and scrabble. Probe: body 0.083, legs 0.056 of a body length inside the ledge.
- **sit** with the near knee told: near-equal, kept. **crouch**: round 10's kept (the spring follow-through lost twice, 55 % each, once with a hook at the end of the tail). **knead**: the original paw formula kept (the redesigned one lost); knead is still the clip judges misread.
- **The carried cat** (`held.ts`, hang tables): forelegs unequal (one nearer the chest), hind legs bent at the knee and unequal, against straight and parallel ones: 5/6/5 against 5/5/4, 60 %. "Still no hock, forelegs at begging height."
- **stretch**: the forepaws' reach at 0.52 popped the hips by 0.043 of a body length in one frame (found by the numbers; the sheet cannot show it), 0.46 pinched the forelegs into a wedge (the 0.52 stretch won that pair, 60 %); shipped 0.50: no pop (0.013 at most), the forelegs near the 0.52 ones.
- **scale** (the card climb, alone): read as "climbing straight up a flat wall seen from behind" (80 %), 6/4/4: forelegs out sideways at shoulder height ("a wide-grip pull-up"), hind legs splayed like a frog's. The forepaws now let go before they pull down to the shoulders (where a rig with no elbow draws a bar), the grips are closer; the numbers are clean (flip 0.03, stretch99 3.4). Judged again on 2026-10-24 as a pair against the version before the change (one fresh judge, sheets unlabeled): both read as "a cat climbing a vertical surface, seen from directly behind" (85 %); marks 7/4/4 for the old arms, **7/5/5 for the shipped ones**, and the shipped ones 60 % more natural (noise-level, but the direction the change was made for: with the old arms the reaching arm vanished and came back as a stub and a planted paw slid about 15 px; with the new ones nothing pops). The judge's list for the shipped clip: the hind recovery is a two-picture flick that ends with the feet splayed like a frog's (spread it over four or five pictures, knees folded under the hips); the reaching arm is not seen until it appears at the ear (it should rise past the shoulder); the lowered arm is a flat flap along the torso; the spine does not flex (compress as the hind legs come up, extend through the push); the tail tip should lag by a picture or two. See Open in `SESSION_LOG.md`.
- **The tails.** A tail told lower than the sit's (150/160/168/176, along the floor) against the sit's (190/185/180/180, out level behind): sit 60 % for the level one, groom 65 % for the lower; a tail wrapped round the haunch to the viewer's side (a yaw of 0.7 to 1.0 on its last pieces) against the level one: **sit 75 % for the level one** ("a rabbit's stub, a dark lump pressed on the haunch"), groom 60 % for the wrap. So the sit keeps its tail level, and the groom has its **own** tail: it lays it down to the floor over a second as the cat settles to wash, flicks the tip, and raises it again (60 % over the level one, noise level; QA clean).
- **What the tail costs the engine.** The shape table the engine plans with is made from pictures of the drawn cat, and the table in the repository was older than the clips: it said a sitting cat's tail reaches 1.11 body lengths behind it, and the pictures of the settled sit say **1.37**, for every tail told lower or level (the table is the most of fourteen pictures, the tail's swish included). Only a wrap shortens it, to 0.89, and the wrap was judged worse. The table was made again (`extents.mjs --write`, now from the settled pictures: a cat that sits, lies down or curls up gets there over a second or more, and the pictures of 1.2 s had the sit's tail still on its way down). The engine's tests count names hidden *with* the table, so they had not been wrong about the engine, only about the picture; their thresholds were measured again and are written in the tests: a told cat hides 0.095 of a body-square of names while it rests on the made-up page (0.078 with the old table), 0.145 of its resting time on the shelf whose names run edge to edge hid more than a tenth of a body-square (0.056), and on the TV library's floor 31 of 138 resting frames did (0 of 568). A third of the cat's sits on the crowded page hide a name; the loaf and the standing cat one in ten; the curled cat never.

## What the judges said again and again (and what became of it)

1. **Dead holds** (every round). No breath, weight shift, ear flick, tail-tip life. Part of this is the sheet (breath and sway live in a layer that each picture restarts), part was the clips: they now have a living hold (`living()` in `acts.mts`: breath, a slow weight shift, a settle) and spring follow-through (`follow.mts`).
2. **Legs with no elbow** (point, groom, tap, stretch, swat). Not fixed. The rig's first leg bone is the whole upper limb (about 0.22 of a body length of a leg 0.38 to 0.4 long) and the next two are short, so there is no true elbow to bend. A different rig or a re-skinned leg is the fix; no clip can make one.
3. **Low postures are a flat slab** (crouch, wiggle, peek, point). Better, not solved: the hunting crouch wants a lower head, a higher rump and a visible fold.
4. **Everything starts and stops together, like a lift** (settling down, the climb). Staggered in the crouch, the loaf and the sit; leading and trailing parts are told apart by the spring follow-through.
5. **Shards of fur on flank, haunch, ruff.** The model's own geometry (identical in every frame), not skinning, not animation. Not changed: the owner's cat is the Meshy model.
6. **The climb**: hinged board, forepaws glued to the wall, hind legs pushing on nothing, a snap to standing. The first climb put the body inside the ledge; the redesign puts the belly to the wall and the forepaw on the lip, scrabbles the hind legs, hooks them over the lip, and carries the tail up before the stand.

## What was not verified

- **No real phone, Fire TV or Safari.** Everything was seen in desktop Chromium through the software renderer (SwiftShader) and in blind sheets. Frame rate and the look of the fur on a real GPU are unknown.
- **The judges are language models looking at stills.** They agree with one another about what is wrong (that is why the lists above are trusted) more than about marks (a mark moves by about a mark between judges). Nobody who is not a model has seen the clips blind.
- **The motion is judged in recordings by me**, who made it.
- **The rear-view climb (`scale`) is wired into the engine** (`World.rearView`, merged from `feat/cat-scale-engine`), tested in the brain and in chaos sessions, and drawn only when the host has the solid cat; it was never seen in the page by anyone, and its arms, changed after its first blind review, were judged again in a last pair on 2026-10-24 (7/5/5 against 7/4/4, 60 % for the new ones: noise-level).
- **Round 13's calls are mostly noise-level** (55 to 65 %), as in rounds 11 and 12. The variants kept are the ones judged at least as good with no extra risk, not ones shown to be better.
- **A mean of 4 to 5 out of 10 is where the clips are**, in pairs and alone. They are read as the right thing (round 6: 12 of 23 named right, 7 nearly, 4 wrong: tap, knead, crouch, wiggle), which the old animation also was. What they do not yet do is look alive in a hold, or bend a leg at an elbow.

## The tests that guard it

- `src/client/cat/render/gl/gl.test.ts`: the clip file is made for this skeleton (another one is refused), the walk carries the cat a stride of the ground a round, the two climbs are there (the ledge climb played once by its progress, the card climb by the distance climbed), the file is under its size budget, the turns face the same way as the one before them, the director keeps every bone's numbers finite and none turned inside out in every posture, walks by the ground covered, passes from one clip to the next without a bone moving further in one picture than a cat can, and says which postures the file has no clip for.
- `src/client/cat/render/climb.test.ts`, `rig.test.ts`, `engine/engine.test.ts`: the climb's marks (`CLIMB_GRAB`, `CLIMB_PULL`, `CLIMB_OVER`) are shared with the cartoon painter, so the climb's timing constants may not change.
- `src/client/cat/engine/shape.test.ts`: the engine plans with how far each posture reaches; after any change to a clip run `node scripts/cat/extents.mjs --write` (and `--check`, which says whether any picture reaches farther than the table; `--clips=<file>` measures a file that is not the one in the app). The engine's tests count the names a resting cat hides **with that table**, so a table that is older than the clips makes them pass for the wrong reason: round 13 found exactly that, and measured them again.
- `src/client/cat/engine/engine.test.ts`, the card climb: the planning on and off, the motion and the hand-over to the side climb, a grab ignored, an abort to a fall, a face covered by a keep-out zone, determinism, and the chaos sessions; and a climb that is begun is begun only where the ledge reaches the place the cat is to stand at (`startClimb` and `runClimb` now agree: a ten-minute session of the engine tests found the cat jumping 267 pixels in one frame (with the new table, seed 1), because the first accepted what the second let go of in the same frame).
