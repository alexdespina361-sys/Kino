# How the cat's clips were checked, and what the checks found

Two kinds of check, and they answer different questions. **The numbers** (`scripts/cat/anim/qa.mts`) say whether a clip is fit to be shown at all: the body holds together, the cat stands on the floor, no bone jumps. **Blind eyes** say whether a clip is any good: a reviewer who is told nothing about it but its length (and whether it loops) sees twelve numbered pictures and says what it is, what is wrong, and marks it. The numbers never said a clip was good; the blind reviews never said a clip was safe. Both were needed, and when they disagreed the pictures won.

## The blind review

- **Who.** Fresh model subagents (Sonnet in the first rounds, Opus from round 3), one per two or three sheets, with no knowledge of the project. Their reports are data: they are quoted here, not obeyed.
- **What they see.** A contact sheet of 12 numbered pictures through the clip (`glsheet.mjs clip --blind=1`), the sheet under a random id, the key (id to clip and variant) kept outside the folder they may open. They are told the clip's length and whether it loops, and the tool's limits: each picture is rendered on its own (so the eyes, ears and tail start afresh in every one), the camera follows the cat (travel is not shown), the thin flaps in the coat are the model's, a tail may be cropped by the edge, a dark panel may be scenery.
- **What they answer.** What the clip is and how sure they are; what is wrong with the body; what is wrong with the motion; marks from 1 to 10 for readability, anatomy and naturalness (10 is a real cat or a studio's work, 5 is a game animation with visible flaws).
- **How it is read.** One judge's mark moves by about a mark from judge to judge, so a mean over a round is read to the nearest half mark and a change smaller than that is noise. From round 11 on, variants are judged as **A/B pairs** (the same clip made two ways, shown in random order, "which is more natural, and how sure are you"); pairs make a judge more lenient (naturalness about 4.7 to 4.9 in pairs against 3.5 when the same kind of clip is judged alone), so a pair says which is better, not how good either is.
- **What it cannot see.** Twelve stills cannot show breath, the lag of a tail, or whether a paw skates. The numbers cover the skating (`gait.mts`: paws slide at most 0.012 of a body length in the walk and the run); breath and lag are looked at in a recording played at speed (`.kino-cat/preview.ps1`, an ffmpeg GIF), by me, not blind.

## The numbers (`qa.mts`)

See the table in the [README](README.md#the-numbers-qamts). Every clip in the shipped file passes the skin numbers (stretch99 at or under about 3.2, flips at or under 0.03, volume between about 0.84 and 1.2, nothing under the floor) except where stated: the climbs carry the highest `hazard` (a share of the mesh blended between two bones turned nearly a half turn apart; the climb's is 0.036) because a cat hanging by its forepaws over a corner winds its limbs as far as the skin can go.

Two tools found what the numbers and the judges did not: `climb-probe` (how far the body and the legs are inside the ledge, in body lengths) found that the first climb had its whole front 0.2 inside the wall (a tube stuck to the corner, no forelegs on the lip, which every judge of round 10 named without being able to say why); the redesigned climb is at 0.083 for the body and 0.046 for the legs. `paw-drift` and `lag` (how far each paw's sole moves in a clip, and how many seconds each part of the body takes to get half way) found that spring follow-through made the tails of short clips late and moved the paws of clips laid on a pose.

### Round 12, what the losses were

- **sit** (round 10 won, 65 %; marks 7/5/5 against 7/4/4): the spring follow-through changed the way the frames before the last were solved, and the viewer's hind leg came to the same fold as the far one, a stub over a stub. A leg is solved from the way it came (the same final posture can end in different knee and hock folds), so the sit now says where the near knee is (`elbow` in `sitSpec`) and the leg lies along the floor.
- **climb** (round 10 won, 60 %; marks 6/5/5 against 7/5/4): the new climb read better (7 against 6, the forepaws on the lip, the belly to the wall) but "moves like a hold and then shoves forward about 35 pixels". The measurement agreed: the hips' speed went to 2.0 to 2.6 body lengths a second in four frames with accelerations of 15 to 22. One key between the pull and the hind legs being over was in the wrong place (0.2 behind the line between its neighbours); moved, the peak speed is 1.8 and the accelerations about half. The engine's marks (`CLIMB_GRAB`, `CLIMB_PULL`, `CLIMB_OVER`) are fixed, so the haul could only be spread inside them.
- **crouch** (round 10 won, 55 %, both 6/5/5): the tail came down in lockstep with the hips (the follow-through's lead plus windows that had been made wide to make up for the lag). The windows were re-measured (the tail got half way at 0.52 s against 0.37 s for the hips).
- **curl** and **peek** were judged last: curl equal (70 %), peek round 12 (70 %, slower, a faint breath, a smaller pop where it wraps).
- Repeated by the judges of curl and peek in both versions: one-picture jumps of the tail tip (13 to 15 pixels), no staggered tuck of the hind legs, no hip roll, a loop that does not flow from its end into its start, no shoulder blades or elbows (the rig).

### Round 13, what was decided

Judged as pairs unless it says "alone"; a call under 65 % is noise, and is written as such.

- **climb**: the heave smoothed (`n3`) beat round 10's (70 %: "a forepaw reads as hooked on the corner, the head ducks and the back rounds as the chest comes over"); the hind foot then told a place a leg can bend to (`n4`, 0.09 of a body length short of the lip, 0.27 under the hips) against `n3`: 55 %, "nearly indistinguishable", the dangling leg a little more flexed. Kept `n4` (not worse, no extra risk). Both judges named the same last fault: the first ~0.4 s is a dead hang that should sag and scrabble. Probe: body 0.083, legs 0.056 of a body length inside the ledge.
- **sit** with the near knee told: near-equal, kept. **crouch**: round 10's kept (the spring follow-through lost twice, 55 % each, once with a hook at the end of the tail). **knead**: the original paw formula kept (the redesigned one lost); knead is still the clip judges misread.
- **The carried cat** (`held.ts`, hang tables): forelegs unequal (one nearer the chest), hind legs bent at the knee and unequal, against straight and parallel ones: 5/6/5 against 5/5/4, 60 %. "Still no hock, forelegs at begging height."
- **stretch**: the forepaws' reach at 0.52 popped the hips by 0.043 of a body length in one frame (found by the numbers; the sheet cannot show it), 0.46 pinched the forelegs into a wedge (the 0.52 stretch won that pair, 60 %); shipped 0.50: no pop (0.013 at most), the forelegs near the 0.52 ones.
- **scale** (the card climb, alone): read as "climbing straight up a flat wall seen from behind" (80 %), 6/4/4: forelegs out sideways at shoulder height ("a wide-grip pull-up"), hind legs splayed like a frog's. The forepaws now let go before they pull down to the shoulders (where a rig with no elbow draws a bar), the grips are closer; the numbers are clean (flip 0.03, stretch99 3.4). Judged again on 2026-10-24 as a pair against the version before the change (one fresh judge, sheets unlabeled): both read as "a cat climbing a vertical surface, seen from directly behind" (85 %); marks 7/4/4 for the old arms, **7/5/5 for the shipped ones**, and the shipped ones 60 % more natural (noise-level, but the direction the change was made for: with the old arms the reaching arm vanished and came back as a stub and a planted paw slid about 15 px; with the new ones nothing pops). The judge's list for the shipped clip: the hind recovery is a two-picture flick that ends with the feet splayed like a frog's (spread it over four or five pictures, knees folded under the hips); the reaching arm is not seen until it appears at the ear (it should rise past the shoulder); the lowered arm is a flat flap along the torso; the spine does not flex (compress as the hind legs come up, extend through the push); the tail tip should lag by a picture or two. See Open in `SESSION_LOG.md`.
- **The tails.** A tail told lower than the sit's (150/160/168/176, along the floor) against the sit's (190/185/180/180, out level behind): sit 60 % for the level one, groom 65 % for the lower; a tail wrapped round the haunch to the viewer's side (a yaw of 0.7 to 1.0 on its last pieces) against the level one: **sit 75 % for the level one** ("a rabbit's stub, a dark lump pressed on the haunch"), groom 60 % for the wrap. So the sit keeps its tail level, and the groom has its **own** tail: it lays it down to the floor over a second as the cat settles to wash, flicks the tip, and raises it again (60 % over the level one, noise level; QA clean).
- **What the tail costs the engine.** The shape table the engine plans with is made from pictures of the drawn cat, and the table in the repository was older than the clips: it said a sitting cat's tail reaches 1.11 body lengths behind it, and the pictures of the settled sit say **1.37**, for every tail told lower or level (the table is the most of fourteen pictures, the tail's swish included). Only a wrap shortens it, to 0.89, and the wrap was judged worse. The table was made again (`extents.mjs --write`, now from the settled pictures: a cat that sits, lies down or curls up gets there over a second or more, and the pictures of 1.2 s had the sit's tail still on its way down). The engine's tests count names hidden *with* the table, so they had not been wrong about the engine, only about the picture; their thresholds were measured again and are written in the tests: a told cat hides 0.095 of a body-square of names while it rests on the made-up page (0.078 with the old table), 0.145 of its resting time on the shelf whose names run edge to edge hid more than a tenth of a body-square (0.056), and on the TV library's floor 31 of 138 resting frames did (0 of 568). A third of the cat's sits on the crowded page hide a name; the loaf and the standing cat one in ten; the curled cat never.

### Round 14, the elbow (2026-10-24): what was tried, what the judges said, why it is not shipped

**What was built.** The model's foreleg is `frontleg` (the shoulder), `frontleg0` (0.22 of a body length away, where the fur of the elbow hangs), `frontleg1`, `frontleg2`: the first bone is the whole upper limb and a raised arm is one straight tube above its last 0.17. (The hind legs already have a thigh, a knee (`backleg0`) and a hock (`backleg1`): nothing was added to them.) A bone `frontleg_e` (`R_frontleg_e`) was hung from `frontleg` half way along it, carrying `frontleg0` and what is below, with no turn of its own; the weights `frontleg` held were shared between the two by a smooth step along the bone (over 0.3 of its length either side of the middle, so the skin does not pinch), then the usual hand-over (`reskin.mts`) as before. The ik (`posegen.mts`) takes a five-bone foreleg (limits on the elbow, on the old elbow, on the wrist; a hint for where the elbow is goes to the new bone, half way from the shoulder), the donors' legs are placed with the five bones (`feet.mts`; the new bone follows no donor bone, it rides with its parent, so the walk, the run and the stand were not meant to change), the held cat's springs have a segment more (`held.ts`, not kept: see below). 36 bones (the shader has room for 40); a full build takes 29 s; the pack is 337,440 B (328,064 B before; the test allows 340 KiB).

Four judges (tap, scale, walk, run) said the sheets looked the same and ran pixel differences of their own: a few hundred pixels a picture, all at the edge of a foreleg, the chest or the tail's tip. Every judge, of both versions, wrote the same last fault: "a tube with no elbow", "no wrist", "a flap from the chest", the torso a rigid barrel, the head frozen, the hind legs gone under the coat in the sit.

**The gate** (the anatomy mark up by 1 or more on most of the clips that show a raised or bent leg, and nothing worse) **was not met**: one clip up by 1, one down (groom, which the new rig makes worse: see below), seven unchanged, two of them (walk, run) unchanged by construction. The shipped pack was left alone. The carried cat was not judged (no sheet of it that a stranger can read was found: the `carried` sheet crops it; the film of a session is the way, and was not made).

**What was learned.**
1. *An elbow shows only where an arm is folded.* In the groom the arm now folds at the elbow and the paw comes up in front of the chest, where the old arm was a long banana to the chin; in an arm that reaches out (the point, the tap, the swat, the stretch, the card climb's) an elbow is a joint that stays nearly straight, and what the viewer sees is the same tube of fur of the same thickness all the way down. The tube is the mesh: the foreleg's coat is one sausage 0.1 to 0.14 of a body length thick from the chest to the paw, and no joint in it makes a forearm. A readable forearm would be a change of the model's shape (a thinner forearm, a bulge at the elbow, a paw), which the owner has said not to touch.
2. *The groom lost because the folded arm leaves the paw at the chest.* With the elbow hint left out (`CAT_ELBOW_PULL=0`) and the joints allowed to close further (`CAT_CLOSE_FRONT=30,90,40`) the paw comes up a little; the picture was looked at but not judged again (the gate cannot be met by one clip).
3. *The walk, the run and the stand are the donors' and were not changed* (the new bone rides with its parent); an elbow would be used by them only if it followed the fox's forearm and the old elbow's bend were moved to it.
4. *The hind legs did not need a hock*: they have one.

**How to build it again** (the shipped mesh and clips are made by the same scripts and are byte for byte what they were: the hashes were compared):
```powershell
$env:CAT_ELBOW="1"; $env:CAT_MESH_OUT="$PWD\.kino-cat\elbow.mesh"
node node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs scripts/cat/model/build-meshy.mts C:\Users\Me\tools\cat-workbench\canonical\Meshy_AI_Fluffy_Blue_Eyed_Cat_All_Animations.glb
node node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs scripts/cat/anim/build.mts --mesh=.kino-cat/elbow.mesh --out=.kino-cat/elbow.clips

```
`CAT_ELBOW_AT` (0.5) and `CAT_ELBOW_ZONE` (0.3) place and soften the elbow; to play it in the app the held cat's springs need a segment more (`held.ts`: the foreleg's `hang`, `fall`, `hz` and `swing` arrays get their first entry twice and the bone's name goes in after `frontleg`; a spec whose bones are not all in the skeleton is skipped), and the shape table has to be made again.

### Round 15, the Sphynx cat as a motion donor (2026-10-24): what was tried, what the judges said, why nothing is shipped

**The donor and the bar.** The donor is "Sphynx Cat" by animalanimator.com (Sketchfab, CC BY, tagged "Generated with AI"; [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)), which the owner saved as `donors\sphynx-cat.glb` (29,997,956 bytes): 107 joints with a real elbow and shoulder blades, and 12 hand-keyed animations at 30 frames a second. It is a thin-limbed cat with a short trunk (0.25 of a body length against the Meshy cat's 0.554, the legs about as long: 0.37 to 0.40 from the hip to the floor). The bar was set before anything was built: a clip ships only if (a) 70 % of its judges prefer it, or its marks are up by one or more with nothing worse (a call of 55 to 65 % and a single mark are noise), and (b) it passes `qa.mts` (stretch99 3.2 or under, flips 0.03 or under, volume 0.9 to 1.2, the climbs' 0.84 to 1.0) and is at least as good as the shipped clip on the numbers; the pack stays under its budget (the shipped one is 328,064 bytes of 340 KiB). No bone's angle is typed in: a clip is the donor's motion on the cat's bones, or a posture told as constraints and solved. (The elbow experiment of round 14 was not repeated.)

**How the motion was put on the cat.** `recipes/sphynx.mts` links the Sphynx's back, neck, head, tail and legs to the cat's, puts the turn of each linked bone in the world on the cat's (`retarget.mts`: the cat's bone is turned by A D A^T, D being the donor's turn since a reference frame and A the donor's frame carried to the cat's), puts the paws where the donor's are (`placeFeet`) and plants those of a gait (`lock`); `build.mts` then finishes it as it finishes every donor (the floor, a gait's stride, a leap's marks, thinning). Two kinds are not a plain transfer: `sphynx-acts.mts` has the groom (the donor's turns on the cat's own sit, the paw solved to the face) and `sphynx-climb.mts` the card climb and the hang of the ledge climb (the cat's own wall posture and keys, moved the way the Sphynx's back, neck, head and tail turn, the paws solved to where the donor's are). The switches are `build.mts --sphynx=groom|scale|climb|stalk|plain-stalk|plain-jump|plain-crouch` (`climb` is the ledge climb; `stalk` is the shipped stalk's head, neck and tail on the Sphynx's paws, hips and back; `plain-...` is the donor's animation as it is) and `--sphynx=off`; `SPHYNX_SHIPPED` in `build.mts` lists the ones that are in the app by default: none, so a plain build is byte for byte the shipped `cat.clips`.

**The pairs.** The Sphynx's version against the shipped clip; fresh Opus judges (two for most pairs, four for the groom's and the ledge climb's), the order swapped within each pair, the sheets under random ids, the key outside their folder. "Preferred by" is the share of a judge's sureness that went to the Sphynx's version in "which is more natural" (a judge who picked the shipped clip at 60 % counts 40). **What the judges said** (quotes are theirs; the shipped clips' faults are here as well, for whoever tries again).
- *Jump.* The shipped clip's air is one held pose ("frames 5 to 8 are one pose, only a forepaw creeps forward"; "the head never turns toward the landing, the spine stays a plank"; "floaty and lifeless in the middle"). The Sphynx's has what it lacks ("the head looks up to launch and down to land, the spine tilts, the recovery crouch is believable") and faults of its own ("the front legs vanish into the chest" in picture 4; "a hind paw stays on the floor throughout, a rear-and-flop in place"; "the neck folds so far that the head sits at paw height"). It is a leap up onto something (the body rears, curls and comes down on its front paws), the shipped one a leap across.
- *Crouch.* The shipped clip sinks "like a lift" into "a flat-bottomed, nearly legless block", the head "pasted on". The Sphynx's reads "a stalk followed by a pounce", and ends with the rump up, the nose down and a paw in the air ("an odd pose to hold for ever"), pictures 5 to 9 nearly the same, the head "buried in the neck fur".
- *Stalk.* The shipped clip is read as a stalk 75 % of the time ("belly low, back level, tail low and straight") but has legs like stubs and "slides as if on rails". The Sphynx's v1 has legs that bend and lift (a hind pad rises 0.16 of a body length, the shipped one's 0.06) but the head and the tail of a walking cat (the head 0.12 of a body length higher against the hips, the tail's tip 0.15 higher) and forepaws curled and lifted half the cycle ("it paddles rather than steps"). v2, the shipped clip's posture on the Sphynx's gait, took tiny steps ("a loaf gliding on a treadmill").
- *Ledge climb.* Three judges of four named the same fault in the shipped hang: in picture 6 one hind leg sticks out straight past the wall's edge. The Sphynx's version bends it, the one thing it changes. Both versions: the hang is frozen for about 0.45 s, the paws are at the chin and the hind legs never visibly push.
- *Card climb.* The Sphynx's: "armless", "a stiff tube", the forelegs "sweep out, in to the head and out again, perfectly mirrored", the hind legs never push ("it flaps rather than climbs"). The shipped one: a frog splay, the hind legs snapping between hanging and flat out to the side, "a human climbing a ladder".
- *Groom.* The shipped clip reads as a groom. The Sphynx's brings the paw to the face by the donor's turns, but the Meshy foreleg has no elbow, the sit has a sack of a torso and the mouth is painted; with the elbow told (v2) it was a toss-up leaning to the shipped clip.

**What was learned.**
1. *A donor of another build brings its own proportions.* The Sphynx's trunk is 0.25 of a body length, the Meshy cat's 0.554: its stalk strides 0.352 body lengths on this cat (the walk's is 0.570) and the engine's stalk speed (0.4 body lengths a second) would play it 1.8 times faster than the donor does. On a low body the legs hide in the coat ("a loaf gliding"). The hybrid did not help (v1 52 %, v2 42.5 %): in v2 the legs went back under the coat ("only stubby paws show under the belly") and the steps were tiny.
2. *The arm solver is chaotic where the arm is taut* (`Body.reach`, the aim just inside its reach): a spec that differed by a hair (the climb's swing taken out) gave elbows bent 30 to 70 degrees differently. A variant of a clip therefore keeps the shipped clip's own spec and copies the legs that must be the same (the ledge climb's forelegs are the shipped climb's to 0.0 degrees).
3. *Compare what is finished the same way.* A scratch candidate has to go through what `build.mts` does (legs brought back to within 3 degrees of the line between their neighbours, thinning) before it is set beside the shipped file: the shipped clip regenerated and finished equals the file to 0.05 degrees. The finish is not harmless on a short loop: on the 22 frames of the card climb it turns the front leg by up to 14 degrees (median 0.006, 99th percentile 5.4), so the card climb that was judged (`--smooth=off --thin=off`) differs from a built one. The verdict did not rest on that: the judges' words are about the whole stroke.
4. *The jump's sheet misleads.* The director shares the engine's progress out over the clip's marks (the take-off at 0.22 of it, the landing at 0.8: `JUMP_TAKEOFF`, `JUMP_LANDING`), so a donor whose take-off is at 1.07 s of 2.4 s is played several times faster before the take-off than a uniform sheet of twelve pictures shows, and the engine tips the cat along its arc, so the donor's own pitch would be added to it. A donor's jump has to be judged with the director's marks (a film of the engine), not on a uniform sheet; this is why the jump went no further.
5. *The crouch is not alone.* The wiggle and the peek are laid on the crouch's posture (`crouchSpec`, and the last frame of `crouch` in `acts.mts`), so a donor's crouch alone would leave their first frame in the old posture. Replacing it means a new `crouchSpec` from the donor's posture that rebuilds the three, and judging the three.
6. *The judges.* All four judges of the ledge climb picked the second sheet they were shown, at 55 to 60 %: the order is swapped within every pair, as it was here. A folder named after the clip (`ledge-hang`) told one judge what it was: the folders are named without it (`ab-q4`).

**What is left** (nothing of it was started).
- *Jump:* the donor's head and spine as a change laid on the shipped jump's keys, judged with the director's marks. The Sphynx's jump has the life the shipped one lacks.
- *Crouch:* the donor's pounce-set posture (head low, rump higher) told as constraints and solved, with a held end pose that is not a rump up with a paw in the air, and the wiggle and the peek laid on it.
- *Ledge climb:* the one real gain is the hind leg of picture 6, which sticks out past the wall's edge in the shipped clip; it can be looked for in the generator's own climb.
- *Groom v3:* a leg path that hugs the chest, a moving paw rather than a parked one, a shorter raised leg, less dive of the head. *Stalk v3:* the plain transfer with the shipped clip's head and tail carried low (I judge it unlikely to reach 70 %, v2 having shown that the legs are the trouble at this body length).
- *Swat:* not made. The donor has no paw swat: its strike ends a pounce (both forepaws, the body surging forward), and the engine places the cat so that one paw lands on the poster 0.62 body lengths ahead of its middle at 0.44 of the clip (`SWAT_REACH`, `SWAT_HIT`), which a pounce would not do without the engine's planning changed.
- *If a clip ever ships from the Sphynx:* its row in SOURCES.md and `source_registry.json` becomes ship-allowed and the credit goes on the cat's settings page beside the Fox's ("Sphynx Cat by animalanimator.com", CC BY, changed to fit, with the link; `cat.settings.credit` in en, ro and it); the TV page has to fit 1280x720 (decision 15 of SESSION_LOG.md).

**What was not checked.** No clip was seen in the app, played by the engine or on a real phone, Fire TV or Safari: everything was judged on sheets of twelve stills drawn by the software renderer, and the frame rate on a real GPU is unknown. Two judges for most pairs means a call under 65 % is noise, as in rounds 11 to 13: the Sphynx's jump and crouch (63.5 %) may be better than the shipped ones, and are not shown to be. The judges are language models looking at stills; nobody who is not a model has seen these clips.

## What the judges said again and again (and what became of it)

1. **Dead holds** (every round). No breath, weight shift, ear flick, tail-tip life. Part of this is the sheet (breath and sway live in a layer that each picture restarts), part was the clips: they now have a living hold (`living()` in `acts.mts`: breath, a slow weight shift, a settle) and spring follow-through (`follow.mts`).
2. **Legs with no elbow** (point, groom, tap, stretch, swat). Not fixed. The rig's first leg bone is the whole upper limb (about 0.22 of a body length of a leg 0.38 to 0.4 long) and the next two are short. Round 14 added an elbow bone to each foreleg (and found that the hind legs have a knee and a hock already) and rebuilt the clips: the judges could not tell the two apart on seven of nine clips, and the one that changed most (the groom) got worse; the fault is the one-piece tube of fur, which a rig does not change. Not shipped. Round 15 put the motion of a cat that has an elbow (the Sphynx) on the bones instead: its groom lost to the shipped one with 7 judges of 8.
3. **Low postures are a flat slab** (crouch, wiggle, peek, point). Better, not solved: the hunting crouch wants a lower head, a higher rump and a visible fold. Round 15 tried the Sphynx's pounce set as the crouch (63.5 %, in the noise, its rump-up end pose "odd to hold for ever"): not shipped; a crouch told from that posture is under "What is left" in round 15.
4. **Everything starts and stops together, like a lift** (settling down, the climb). Staggered in the crouch, the loaf and the sit; leading and trailing parts are told apart by the spring follow-through.
5. **Shards of fur on flank, haunch, ruff.** The model's own geometry (identical in every frame), not skinning, not animation. Not changed: the owner's cat is the Meshy model.
6. **The climb**: hinged board, forepaws glued to the wall, hind legs pushing on nothing, a snap to standing. The first climb put the body inside the ledge; the redesign puts the belly to the wall and the forepaw on the lip, scrabbles the hind legs, hooks them over the lip, and carries the tail up before the stand.

## What was not verified

- **No real phone, Fire TV or Safari.** Everything was seen in desktop Chromium through the software renderer (SwiftShader) and in blind sheets. Frame rate and the look of the fur on a real GPU are unknown.
- **The judges are language models looking at stills.** They agree with one another about what is wrong (that is why the lists above are trusted) more than about marks (a mark moves by about a mark between judges). Nobody who is not a model has seen the clips blind.
- **The motion is judged in recordings by me**, who made it.
- **The rear-view climb (`scale`) is wired into the engine** (`World.rearView`, merged from `feat/cat-scale-engine`), tested in the brain and in chaos sessions, and drawn only when the host has the solid cat; it was never seen in the page by anyone, and its arms, changed after its first blind review, were judged again in a last pair on 2026-10-24 (7/5/5 against 7/4/4, 60 % for the new ones: noise-level).
- **Round 13's calls are mostly noise-level** (55 to 65 %), as in rounds 11 and 12. The variants kept are the ones judged at least as good with no extra risk, not ones shown to be better.
- **A mean of 4 to 5 out of 10 is where the clips are**, in pairs and alone. They are read as the right thing (round 6: 12 of 23 named right, 7 nearly, 4 wrong: tap, knead, crouch, wiggle), which the old animation also was. What they do not yet do is look alive in a hold, or bend a leg at an elbow.

## The tests that guard it

- `src/client/cat/render/gl/gl.test.ts`: the clip file is made for this skeleton (another one is refused), the walk carries the cat a stride of the ground a round, the two climbs are there (the ledge climb played once by its progress, the card climb by the distance climbed), the file is under its size budget, the turns face the same way as the one before them, the director keeps every bone's numbers finite and none turned inside out in every posture, walks by the ground covered, passes from one clip to the next without a bone moving further in one picture than a cat can, and says which postures the file has no clip for.
- `src/client/cat/render/climb.test.ts`, `rig.test.ts`, `engine/engine.test.ts`: the climb's marks (`CLIMB_GRAB`, `CLIMB_PULL`, `CLIMB_OVER`) are shared with the cartoon painter, so the climb's timing constants may not change.
- `src/client/cat/engine/shape.test.ts`: the engine plans with how far each posture reaches; after any change to a clip run `node scripts/cat/extents.mjs --write` (and `--check`, which says whether any picture reaches farther than the table; `--clips=<file>` measures a file that is not the one in the app). The engine's tests count the names a resting cat hides **with that table**, so a table that is older than the clips makes them pass for the wrong reason: round 13 found exactly that, and measured them again.
- `src/client/cat/engine/engine.test.ts`, the card climb: the planning on and off, the motion and the hand-over to the side climb, a grab ignored, an abort to a fall, a face covered by a keep-out zone, determinism, and the chaos sessions; and a climb that is begun is begun only where the ledge reaches the place the cat is to stand at (`startClimb` and `runClimb` now agree: a ten-minute session of the engine tests found the cat jumping 267 pixels in one frame (with the new table, seed 1), because the first accepted what the second let go of in the same frame).
