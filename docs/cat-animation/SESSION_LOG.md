# Session log: the cat's animation, 2026-10-23 to 2026-10-24

What was decided, in order, and what bit on the way, for whoever continues. The result of each blind review is in [QA.md](QA.md); where each source comes from is in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## The brief

The owner called the detailed cat's animation "very unpleasant" and scored it 4 of 10 for naturalness, said the cat looks a bit off against the original model, and asked for the animation to be **made by tools, data or a better model** and not keyed by hand again. The standing plan: the Meshy cat stays canonical; nothing paid; every source in a ledger as ship-allowed, reference-only or rejected; a clip is "good" only if blind reviewers give it 4 or more for readability, anatomy and timing; paws planted, no skating; the player and the TV remote are not disturbed.

## Decisions

1. **Where the motion comes from.** A donor's hand-made animation is put on the cat's bones by what each bone does in the world (`retarget.mts`, `recipes/`): the Mesh2Motion fox for the walk, run and yawn, the Khronos Fox for the stand. Everything no donor does is a **posture told as constraints and solved for the bones** (`posegen.mts`, `acts.mts`). No bone's angle is typed in.
2. **What was looked at and not used.** MoCapAnything (arXiv 2604.28130, motion capture of any skeleton from video): its own reported joint error, about 23 degrees, is more than a leg chain takes. A search for a better cat model (Objaverse, Quaternius, Poly Pizza, Wikimedia, OpenGameArt): none came near the Meshy cat. Quaternius cats: too few bones to drive this cat's legs. Furever, a commercial cat app: never run, nothing of it used. The Sphynx cat of Sketchfab (CC BY, sign-in needed): the file did not come at first; it came later and was tried as a motion donor, see decision 18.
3. **Skinning.** Dual quaternions bulge at sharply bent shoulders, elbows and haunches; linear blending creases there. The player blends the two (`SKIN_LINEAR` 0.7). A sign bug in the dual-quaternion blend was found on the way and fixed.
4. **The clip file's sign convention** (`clipfile.ts`): the turns of a bone face the same way as the one before, and the first faces the way the bone's rest does, so that a fade from one clip into another does not flip the sign of a bone where the fade ends.
5. **Floor.** The whole cat is moved so that its lowest vertex is at the floor; a tail that would go through it is clamped (`tailFloor`); a gait's feet are planted (`plant.mts`), the steps staggered.
6. **Limits on each leg bone** (`normal`, `wide`, `free`): `normal` made the skin numbers clean and the pictures worse (round 9), because it kept a low cat on its feet. The low postures and the stretch use `wide`, the card climb `free`.
7. **Smoothing and thinning** (`smooth.mts`, `thin.mts`): a leg that is reached for each frame on its own flicks; each frame that leaves the line between its neighbours by more than `--smooth` degrees (3 by default) is pulled back. Frames that the player's own interpolation reproduces to within `--tol` degrees (0.5) are dropped.
8. **Mouth.** The jaw was a flat red patch ("red lipstick"); it is a shader now (`groom` once read as "horrifying" for the inflated body, the shards at the haunch, the sausage of an arm and that mouth; the body table and the mouth fixed those).
9. **Follow-through** (`follow.mts`): the hips, the back, the neck and the tail are each a spring that follows what the clip says, slower the further out they are, so a part leads and a part trails and nothing starts and stops like a lift. A clip told "where to be in `LEAD` seconds" (`ahead`) is not late at its end. `living()` in `acts.mts` lays a breath and a slow weight shift over a hold and plants every paw the clip does not move (a body that moves over a paw that was not told to stay makes the paw drift: measured 0.02 to 0.1 of a body length).
10. **The ledge climb** was redesigned from measurement, not from looking: the cat's belly is 0.24 of a body length deep, so a spine at the wall puts the front inside it. The probe (`climb-probe`) reported the first climb 0.27 inside; the keys now keep the belly to the wall as far as the corner, the forepaw on the lip, the hind legs scrabbling and then hooking over, and end at 0.083 (body) and 0.046 (legs).
11. **The card climb, from behind** (`scale`): the user's request ("when I say cat climbs, it doesn't mean only from the side"). A bound gait played by the distance climbed (the clip's `stride`), seen from behind. The renderer plays it (`director.ts`); the engine wiring (`feat/cat-scale-engine`: `World.rearView`, the `scale` act that hands over to the side climb) is merged.
12. **The tails** (round 13). A tail told lower is not a tail that reaches less: every sit measured 1.35 to 1.37 body lengths behind the feet, whether it was told 190/185/180/180, 150/160/168/176, 165/168/172/178 or 175/172/172/176 (the tail is a brush 0.25 to 0.3 of a body length thick, and the table is the most of fourteen pictures, the swish of the tail included). Only a wrap round the haunch by a yaw (0.7, 1.0, 1.0 on the last three pieces) shortens it, to 0.89, and a blind judge gave 75 % to the level tail ("a rabbit's stub, a dark lump on the thigh"). The sit keeps its tail out level behind it. The groom, for which a lowered tail won twice (65 %, 60 %), lays its own down to the floor as the cat settles to wash (`GROOM_TAIL` in `acts.mts`, a spec of its own over a second), flicks the tip, and raises it again before the loop closes. A tail's angle in a spec is larger for higher in the picture, and `anglesOf` gives it by `atan2` (-170 degrees for a tail told 190), so a mix of two tails has to take the turn the short way (a first build mixed them through the wrong side: a pop of 180 degrees, found by the numbers).
13. **The table of the cat's reach was older than the clips** (round 13). `src/client/cat/engine/shape-table.ts` is made from pictures of the drawn cat (`extents.mjs --write`) and the engine keeps the cat off the words by it. The table in the repository said the sit reaches 1.11 body lengths behind it; the pictures of the settled sit say 1.37 (the old table was made from pictures at 1.2 s, with the tail still on its way down, and before the clips changed). `extents.mjs` had also gone stale (it called the picture maker with the old arguments); it reads `--clips=<file>` now, the pictures of a posture that takes a second or more to get into are of the cat settled in it (`SETTLED` in `lab/gl.ts`), and the card climb is in the table. The engine's tests count hidden names with the table, so they passed for the wrong reason; they were measured again (see QA.md) and their numbers are written in them. What the honest table means for the cat: a sitting cat hides a name in a third of its resting time on a page whose names run edge to edge, and the engine has fewer places to sit on a crowded shelf.
14. **`startClimb` and `runClimb` agreed** (round 13). The first took a ledge by `ledgeAt` (the nearest of that surface, however far), the second let go of one that did not reach 0.2 of a body length either side of the place the cat was to stand at; a climb whose ledge had been cut by the page (a zone, a resized shelf) began, was let go of in the same frame, and the cat, already moved to the top of the climb, faded out from there (a 267-pixel jump in one frame, found by a session test once the table changed what was cut). `startClimb` now refuses the climb by the same rule.
15. **The end-to-end spec** (`e2e/cat.spec.ts`, 63 tests) had not been run on this branch; the first run: 61 passed, 2 failed, each for a cause. (a) The cat's page of the settings was 8 to 29 px taller than a TV's screen (1280x720, 1366x768 and 1920x1080, in English and Italian): the credit of the Fox's animation (CC BY asks for it, so it stays in full) and a longer help text had been added to the left column. The credit is in the right column now, under the look it is the credit of, and a step smaller (`.tv-set-credit`); the page fits in the three languages at the four screen sizes of the test with 14 px or more to spare. (b) "a page that is flung..." failed about one run in nine: a call that lands while the cat is starting a leap of its own (decision 16); the test called again (`callTo`) until the engine was mended, and calls once, as it did, now. Two more tests failed at random in the later full runs: "a hand that lets the cat go just under the edge of a shelf" (two runs in forty-five; none in thirty traced runs; its check of how low the cat hangs is 0.15 of a body length tight) and "a white and grey cat that is drawn simply..." (three runs in fourteen: the first retry is still fetching the cat's files when the test tells the browser to give the graphics, and that try gets them): the second now lets the first try end (thirty of thirty after). The last full run: 63 passed. The first of the two is flaky still, at a few runs in a hundred.
16. **A call that lands in the middle of a leap** (the engine, not the animation; older than this work, but the honest shape table makes the cat move about more, so it showed more). `comeTo` made its route at once, from the shelf the cat stands on, and `hardPlan` lets a leap finish first (winding up, about 0.3 s on the ground; in the air; settling), and the shelf the cat stands on changes only at touchdown: the cat landed on another shelf and walked a route that was made for the one it left (the wrong row, an x that belongs to the other shelf, and a sit there). The end-to-end test "a page that is flung, ..." failed about one run in nine for it (a trace: `shot:peek`, the cat's own leap down from row 23 to row 25, the call's plan queued behind it, a walk to x = 775 on row 25 and a sit). `playPlan` (the command to play) had the same shape with a milder end: the stale hops are dropped at the first one the cat cannot make from where it landed (`startJump` empties the queue), so the play was cut short. Now `comeTo` (a call, and the command to come, which is a call to where the pointer is) and `playPlan` hold what they were told while the cat cannot set out (`readyToSetOut`: not placed, leaving, in a leap, a climb, a card, a hand or a fall, or on a shelf the page no longer has) in `pendingCall` (`EngineDebug.asked`: `come` or `play`), and `next()` answers it from where the cat is when it can, as it answers `pendingPoint` for a poster and with the same limit (`POINT_WAIT`, 8 s from the call; a cat that still cannot set out waits some more, against the first deadline). A later call, command, point or grab replaces it; a page that goes away or to a film, a cat that is sent away and a viewer who asks for less motion drop it (answering it would have put a sit over the cat's fade-out). The rule is kept that `cat.call` and `cat.command` are ignored while the cat is unreachable (away, leaving, not placed) or in a hand (carried, fall, climb, scale): such a call is not held. The other places that replace the plan were read for the same fault and need nothing: a stroke, a flinch and the hop of joy already stand off a cat in a leap, the ride on a row of posters is off in one, and the step out of a keep-out zone and the walk off a shelf that has grown small go along the shelf under the cat's feet and are not routes. The tests are in `engine.test.ts` ("the cat called, or told to play, in the middle of a leap of its own", 6): a call in the crouch, in the air and in the settle (four seeds each: the cat must stand on the shelves of the right route, `a`, `b`, `d`, `c`, and sit where it was called), the command to come, the command to play (held, then played from the shelf it landed on), what makes the call forgotten (a later command to sit; a poster to show, which it goes to from the shelf it lands on; the page going away) and a cat in a hand (not held). All six fail on the engine as it was: the first two on the cat's route (it stays on `b` where it should have gone on to `c`), the other four only because they read the hold itself (`debug().asked`), which that engine does not have (the command to play, on that engine, plays two hops where it should play three on some seeds, and the same hops on the others: its cost was small). And the end-to-end test calls once again and was run twenty times in a row: twenty passed. The 418 unit tests of the cat pass, the whole end-to-end spec passes (63 of 63, 9 minutes), and so does the soak (seeds 13-112, all 18 kinds, 194 s: none found; the hold is entered about once in four ten-minute sessions, 5 times in 20 sessions of the first kind, 4 of them a call and 1 a command to play, so the soak does put it to work).

17. **The elbow, tried and not shipped** (2026-10-24, round 14 of QA.md). The rig's missing elbow was the judges' most repeated fault, so a bone was added half way along each foreleg's first bone, the weights shared along it, the ik and the donors' legs given five bones, and all 24 clips rebuilt (36 bones; 337,440 B). Nine blind A/B pairs: one clip up by 1 (point), one down (groom), seven equal; the judges could mostly not tell the sheets apart. The gate (the anatomy mark up by 1 on most of the clips with a raised or bent leg, and nothing worse) was not met, so the shipped mesh and clips are untouched. What stays: the experiment behind `CAT_ELBOW=1` in `build-meshy.mts` (default off: the mesh and the clips rebuild byte for byte as they were), a five-bone leg in `posegen.mts` and `feet.mts` that is used only when the skeleton has the bone (`legsOf`), the recipes' chains with the new bone in them (the bone is skipped when the skeleton has not got it), `qa.mts --mesh=`, and the write-up. Traps met: the new bone must be named with `leg` in it (`smooth.mts` smooths the bones that match `/leg/`); the elbow hint's place is for the model's old elbow (0.22 from the shoulder) and is scaled to half for the new bone; `CAT_ELBOW_PULL` multiplies the hint's weight for every leg, the hind legs' knee hint of the sit included (the sit changes with it); a limit on a joint is the angle between the lines to its neighbours, and a five-bone leg has two joints close together at the elbow (the old elbow, 33 degrees bent at rest, is limited to 50 degrees closing, 125 for the one the model had).
18. **The Sphynx cat as a motion donor, tried and not shipped** (2026-10-24, round 15 of QA.md). The owner got the Sphynx cat (CC BY; 107 joints with a real elbow; 12 hand-keyed animations) as `donors/sphynx-cat.glb` and asked for it to be tried on the clips the judges liked least. The bar was fixed first: a clip ships only if 70 % of its judges prefer it (or its marks are up with nothing worse) and it passes `qa.mts` and is as good as the shipped clip on the numbers. Then six clips were made from it, one at a time and each judged as blind A/B pairs before the next: the groom, the card climb, the ledge climb, the jump, the stalk and the crouch (the swat was not made: the donor has none). The motion is the donor's world turns put on the cat's bones with the paws solved to where its paws are (`recipes/sphynx.mts`, `sphynx-acts.mts`, `sphynx-climb.mts`); no angle is typed. The result: the shipped clip won three (the groom with 7 judges of 8, the card climb 72 and 65 %, the stalk 52 % and 42.5 % for the Sphynx), the ledge climb is a tie (two judges each way, every mark equal), and two went to the Sphynx in the noise (the jump and the crouch, 63.5 % on average, with a mark down in the pairs; the jump also fails the skin gates). **Nothing is shipped**: the pack, the mesh, the engine and `dist/client` are what they were, the default build is byte for byte the shipped `cat.clips`, and the recipe and its switches (`build.mts --sphynx=...`) stay, each judged version rebuilds byte for byte. What the Sphynx gave is real and small: legs that bend and lift in a gait, life in the head and the spine of a jump, a hind leg at the ledge that does not stick out; what the judges read first is the outline (a low stalk, a belly to the wall), which the shipped clips have, and a donor with a trunk of 0.25 of a body length against this cat's 0.554 takes tiny strides on it. Traps met: the arm solver (`Body.reach`) is chaotic where the arm is taut (a spec that differs by a hair gave elbows bent 30 to 70 degrees differently), so a variant keeps the shipped clip's spec and copies the legs that must not change; a scratch candidate must be finished as `build.mts` finishes it before it is set beside the shipped file (the finish turns the front leg of the 22-frame card climb by up to 14 degrees); a donor's jump is played by the engine's progress through the director's anchors, so a uniform sheet of it misleads; the crouch, the wiggle and the peek stand on one posture (`crouchSpec`), so one cannot be replaced alone; a judge shown two sheets prefers the second a little (all four ledge judges, 55 to 60 %), so the order is swapped within a pair, and a folder named after the clip tells the judge what it is.

## Traps (each cost time once)

- `tsc -p . --noEmit` does not cover `scripts/`; the scripts are checked by `scripts/cat/anim` through `.kino-cat/tsconfig.scripts.json` (not tracked) and run with `node node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs`.
- Importing a script from a scratch folder needs a `file:///` URL.
- A `Timing` in `between(a, b, t, timing)` is `[start seconds, duration seconds]`, not `[start, end]`.
- `hips.pitch` is the pelvis angle **minus** its rest angle (about -12 degrees), not the angle.
- The film strip does not draw a recording's first picture; the preview starts at the second.
- The sheet maker once shared one clock across the pictures of a sheet; clock-driven clips looked like "a plop and then a freeze" in round 3. Fixed.
- `--smooth` is a limit on **curvature**: a leg that really swings fast at 30 frames a second leaves the line between its neighbours by degrees, so a small limit would blunt a kneading paw or a lick. Do not lower it to make a clip thin better.
- A clip with a living hold moves 23 to 27 bones in every frame and cannot be thinned to a lower frame rate within half a degree: the file is about 320 KB, not the 240 KB it was before the holds lived.
- Judges are noisy by about a mark; compare two versions as an A/B pair, and do not read a mark out of a pair. A judge shown two sheets prefers the second a little (round 15: all four ledge judges, at 55 to 60 %), so swap the order within every pair; and a folder named after the clip tells the judge what it is (`ledge-hang`): name the folders `ab-q4`.
- The clip file is read by the shape table (`extents.mjs --write`) and shares the climb's marks with the cartoon painter: the engine's climb constants (`CLIMB_*`) may not change.
- The engine's tests that count hidden names use the shape table, so a table older than the clips lets them pass; after any change to a clip, make the table again (`extents.mjs --write`, then `--check`) **before** reading the tests.
- A wide posture is cropped by the sheet's cell (`glsheet.mjs ... --cellw=560` for the sit with its tail); a cell of 260 hides the tail and the footprint looks smaller than it is.
- `pnpm build`, which Playwright's `webServer` runs, wants to reinstall `node_modules` when the folder is a junction to another worktree's, and stops with `ERR_PNPM_ABORTED_REMOVE_MODULES_DIR_NO_TTY` (no terminal): never `pnpm install`. Run the end-to-end tests with a config of one's own whose `webServer` is `node node_modules/vite/bin/vite.js build && node node_modules/tsx/dist/cli.mjs src/server/index.ts` and `cwd: process.cwd()` (a web server starts in the folder of its config, and `vite` is not found there). Start a long run with the tool's background flag, not `Start-Job` (the job died with the call). After a few hundred page loads in a row Windows can answer `net::ERR_NO_BUFFER_SPACE`: not the cat.
- On this Windows machine the Bash tool fails (`ENAMETOOLONG`): everything runs in PowerShell. `Remove-Item` of a file that is not there aborts a whole command, and the files of the repository are CRLF (a replace that inserts a bare `\n` leaves mixed endings: check with a regex).

## Open

What is left is the shape of the coat: a foreleg that is one sausage of fur from the chest to the paw, so a raised foreleg is a tube whatever its joints. A forearm that reads needs the mesh changed (thinner below the elbow, a bulge at it), which the owner has not asked for; the unfinished options are a re-sculpted foreleg of the Meshy model, or the Sphynx GLB (a thin-limbed cat whose legs show) as the model and not only as a donor of motion (round 15 and decision 18 used it for motion only, on the Meshy cat).
- **Low postures** (the hunting crouch, the peek, the wiggle) still read as a flat slab: a lower head, a higher rump and a visible fold are wanted. (Round 15's Sphynx pounce set, put on the crouch as it is, was 63.5 % for it and ended in a rump up with a paw in the air; a crouch told from that posture, with the wiggle and the peek laid on it, is not made: QA.md, round 15, "What is left".)
- **Knead** snaps where a paw takes over (pictures 4 to 5 and 10 to 11, in both versions judged); the **stretch**'s front paws slide at the bottom; the **sit**'s rump dips through the floor for a frame at its end.
- **Nothing was seen on a real phone, Fire TV or Safari.** The rear-view climb is played by the engine (`World.rearView`) and was never seen in a page; its arms changed after its first blind review and the new ones were judged in a last pair (7/5/5 against 7/4/4, 60 % for the new ones: see QA.md).
- **The ledge climb's first 0.4 s is a dead hang** (both judges of round 13): it should sag, with the hind feet scrabbling on the wall and the elbows loading. The engine's marks (`CLIMB_GRAB` 0.20) bound how early the pull can start. (Round 15 put the Sphynx's swim on the hang: both versions were still "frozen for about 0.45 s" to the judges, the paws at the chin and the hind legs not visibly pushing.)
- **The sit's tail is 1.37 body lengths behind it** and a wrap that shortens it reads as a stub (75 % against): if names hidden by a sitting cat matter more than the look of its tail, tell the sit's tail shorter or lower in the picture by another way (a tail that is simply shorter in the mesh) and make the table again.
- **The carried cat** still has no hock and its forelegs hang at begging height (round 13 judge): a tucked variant (forelegs down against the ribs, hind legs tucked or a limp zigzag, tail curled forward between the hind legs) was named by the judge and not made.
- **Knead** is read as something else by judges (round 6 and since): it needs a redesign for readability, which was not done.
- **The card climb (`scale`)**, the last judge's list for the clip that ships (2026-10-24): the hind recovery is a two-picture flick that ends with the feet splayed like a frog's (spread it over four or five pictures, knees folded under the hips); the reaching arm is not seen until it appears at the ear (it should rise past the shoulder); the lowered arm is a flat flap along the torso (tuck it under the chest or weight it differently); the spine does not flex (compress as the hind legs come up, extend through the push); the tail tip should lag by a picture or two. None of it done.
- The fur looks as the model made it (flat white shards at some edges). Flat lighting would hide them; not done, because the owner's cat is the Meshy model as it is.
