# The cat companion: an opt-in cat that lives on the TV's screen

Built in five rounds: `feat/kino-cat` (the first cat), `feat/kino-cat-2` (the detailed cat, PRs #28/#29), `feat/kino-cat-3` (exactly two cats to choose from, the cat that goes to the title it is asked to find and swats at it, the cat that reacts when its row is scrolled, and a few more things it does of its own accord; PR #30), `feat/kino-cat-4` (a follow-up: the sleeping pose drawn as a loaf, a cat that falls when the page is flung, and a cat that keeps its shelf without steps while the page scrolls) and `feat/cat-5-everywhere` (the cat reads every word of a page and no longer walks through them, lives on every page of the TV and not only the library, is a layer over the film's controls that a hand can always take, and has a right-click menu whose "Send to sleep" takes it away until it is woken; see "Round 5" below). **Off for everybody until the host sets `CAT_COMPANION=on` and a screen's owner turns it on.** The long reports (research, renderer choice, test matrix, risks, journal) are in `reports/kino-cat/`; this page is the short version for whoever works on the site.

## What was asked

An original animated cat for Kino, in the spirit of a desktop pet but for a streaming site: it walks on the objects of the page (the rows of posters), rests, sleeps, can be petted and called, finds a title on request, lounges in a film without covering the controls, the subtitles or the remote's focus, and never starts anything. The owner then asked for a more realistic, more detailed cat than the first, flat one (2-D or 2.5-D both fine), gave a reference picture (a fluffy blue-eyed bicolour ragdoll) and, in the end, a free model to build it from: **the detailed cat's body is made from "Fluffy Blue-Eyed Cat" by Meshy AI (CC BY 4.0, named in the cat's settings page and in `reports/kino-cat/06-risk-and-license-register.md`)**, with the skeleton, the animations, the eyes and the mouth written here. Furever Dock, a commercial desktop pet, was a research reference only: nothing of it is in the repository (`reports/kino-cat/01-reference-analysis.md`, `06-risk-and-license-register.md`).

## What it does

| Where | The cat |
|---|---|
| The library (`/tv`, a computer's browser, a phone used as the screen) | walks along the tops of the posters, leaps to other rows, sits, lies, sleeps, washes, stretches, yawns, scratches, kneads, sniffs, stalks a poster and pounces, goes up to a poster on its shelf and pats it (the poster gives a little), now and then has the zoomies (runs and leaps from shelf to shelf, then sits down, pleased with itself); looks at the card that has the remote's focus, and goes over to sit by it when that is on another shelf; comes to a pointer that has rested, and visits a poster on another shelf; does not stay at the foot of the screen when the page has shelves (it is on one again in a few seconds: see "Going about"); keeps out of words, buttons and that card, and settles, when the shelf has such a place, where its body does not cover the names under the cards |
| The viewer scrolls the row the cat stands on | the posters slide under it and it goes with them (it is on them); when they have carried it a good way (1.2 body lengths) it hops back to where it was, or, now and then (35 %), away to another row, and sits. A cat that is quiet, or for a viewer who wants less motion, only goes with the posters and does not hop. A page scrolled up and down, so that another shelf is where its feet are, makes it stand on that shelf (it does not leap off for nothing) |
| The viewer scrolls the page fast or far (a key held down, a page key, a flung wheel, a wheel spun, a scrollbar dragged, two steps of the remote one on the other's heels) | the page does not take the cat up with its shelf: when the shelves go up by more than a step and a quarter of the page's own rows (1.25 pitches inside half a second, at more than 12 body lengths a second), or faster than 40 body lengths a second in two readings of the page in a row, the cat is shaken off and **falls**, in front of the page that goes by (it seems to hang there while the page flies, and comes down), and lands on the first place under it once the page has slowed down (under 8 body lengths a second), or on the foot of the screen. One step of the remote (the first from the top of the library too, which folds the banner first), a second step after a pause, or a notch or two of the wheel still takes it with its shelf |
| The page scrolls under the cat, however slow the screen | the cat goes with its shelf at every frame, exactly (the page is read now and then, but where it has been scrolled to is asked at every frame: `host/carry.ts`), and a screen the governor has put on thirty pictures a second still moves the cat's place sixty times a second while the rows go by (`follow`, `host/index.ts`) |
| A title's page, the settings and each of its pages, the lists to choose, the sign-in and party pages | the cat is there, over the page, walking its boxes and the lines in front of its words; a page taller than the screen scrolls and the places the cat has on it go with it (round 5, below). A genuine modal (the feedback form, the party panel, a film's menu, the first-run welcome), a page laid over a film, and a page that only says something is on its way send it away, and it is back when they close |
| A film | rests at the foot of the picture, between the groups of the controls (on a screen too narrow for that, in a corner of the picture on the line where the bars begin), in a layer **over the controls' bars and under the subtitles and what the player puts up in passing** (a note, the next episode's card, a menu): it is never over a button or a word, and none of them makes it come or go; a device can say "no cat in films". **A hand can take it there, with the controls up or not**: only the cat's own body takes a press (about 89 by 43 px at 1280 by 720, 62 by 30 at 390 wide), every other press on the picture is the player's as ever (a tap, a double-tap to skip, a hold to speed up, the seek bar), and a press on the cat starts none of them. Dropped, it falls to the film's perch at that spot |
| The corner button (cat's head, beside the bell and the profile) | opens its panel: come here, pet, sit, sleep, play, go away, and **find me something**: the cat picks a title that is on the page, by the mood and the shelf names (en/ro/it); the poster glows and the cat **goes to it** (see "Finding a title" below): hops along the poster's own shelf, creeps up on it, swats at it with a paw (the poster rocks under the paw), points, and sits and glances at the viewer; "Open it" opens its page like OK on the poster; nothing is ever played |
| Settings → Cat companion (its own page, only where the host offers a cat) | on, quiet (it rests where it is: a TV starts with this on), films, **which cat: exactly two, the white and grey ragdoll (the detailed one, the default) and the ginger cartoon cat**, size; when the ragdoll was chosen and the screen can only give the simple version, a line that says what is drawn and why (the screen was too slow, no WebGL, the file did not come, the browser took the picture away) and a "try again" button; and the model's credit ("Fluffy Blue-Eyed Cat" by Meshy AI, CC BY 4.0) |
| A mouse resting on the cat | strokes it |
| A mouse pressed on the cat and moved (more than 8 px) | picks it up by the scruff: it hangs from the hand (the body slung from the nape, hind legs and tail down, forelegs tucked, a slow sway about the hand), follows the hand and shows, as a soft ellipse on the shelf, where letting go would put it (in a film too: over the picture and the controls). **Held a couple of seconds it begins to fight the hand** (see "Held by the scruff" below): it twists, kicks with its hind legs, swipes with a forepaw, its ears go back and its mouth opens; it tires and goes limp, and tries again, a little less hard each time |
| The hand lets go | the cat falls to that shelf (or the nearest one it can reach; in a film, to the film's perch at that spot), lands, and sits or washes; **one that was fighting the hand lands annoyed instead**: it flicks its tail and walks off, or washes as if it had meant to be there, or is startled and bolts, or shakes itself from head to tail. **When the top edge of a shelf is above its paws by more than it can spring up to (0.6 body lengths) and by no more than a stretched body (1.65), and a little across, it does not fall: it reaches for the edge, takes hold with its forepaws, hangs a moment and hauls itself up onto the shelf** (see "Reaching and climbing" below). A hand that is lost (a window that loses focus, a tab that is hidden, a pointer that vanishes) lets it go too, and so does 2.5 s of silence |
| A press that only wobbles (a stroke), `prefers-reduced-motion`, quiet | lifts nothing; the cursor does not offer a grab or close on the cat |
| A second hand (a finger) while the mouse has the cat | changes nothing: the first hand keeps the cat until it lets go |
| A right click on the cat, or a finger resting on it for 0.55 s | **its menu**, a small card beside it: Pet, Come here (not in a film: it rests where it is there), Send to sleep. It takes no focus and no key, and none of its presses reaches the player under it. Back (Escape) closes only the menu; a click or touch anywhere else closes it and is *only* that (no tap on the picture, no click on a poster); any other key closes it and is the page's; so do a scroll, the window changing, another page, or a film beginning. The phone's own Back closes it. It is not in the tab order: the corner button's panel is how a remote, a keyboard and a screen reader reach the same things |
| Send to sleep (the menu), Send away (the panel) | the cat curls up where it is (for a viewer who wants less motion: no curling) and fades out, and **stays away on every page and in every film until it is woken**. The corner button's panel then says "The cat is asleep." and offers "Wake the cat"; the cat's page in Settings has the same row (and the remote is put on the switch above it when it is pressed); switching the cat off and on again, or loading the page again, wakes it too. It is for one load of the page: nothing is stored |
| `prefers-reduced-motion` | the cat only rests, in one place |

The phone as a *remote* has no cat. The cat lives on the screen that plays.

## Gates and the kill switch (all must be open)

| Gate | Default | How |
|---|---|---|
| The host offers it | off | environment variable `CAT_COMPANION=on` → `GET /api/features` `{cat:true}` (also `/api/health` `features.cat`). **Unset it and every screen loses its cat at its next load**; nothing is stored on the server |
| The screen's role | the screen that plays only | automatic |
| The device asks for it | off | Settings → Cat companion (`kino.cat` in the device's own storage), or `?cat=on` for one load |
| (testing) address | – | `?cat=on|off|debug`, `?catseed=N`, `?catbudget=ms`, `?catslow=ms`, `?catart=classic|solid`, `?catcoat=` (the settings offer the two cats; the address can still ask for the tuxedo, grey tabby and calico coats, to look at them), `?catsize=` |

Nothing of the cat is downloaded before the gates are open: the always-loaded part is the switch, the corner button, the loader and the texts (+6.6 KB gzip over the whole app: the scripts of `/tv` were 127.6 KB gzip on `main` after PR #27 and are 134.2 KB now); the cat is a lazy chunk (`host-*.js`, 51 KB gzip, plus a 1.7 KB stylesheet) and, for the detailed cat, one mesh file (`cat-*.mesh`, 165 KB gzip), fetched once.

## Two cats, one governor

- **Detailed cat** (WebGL1): a skinned model (`scripts/cat/model/build-meshy.mts` makes `cat.mesh` offline from the free Meshy AI model, which is *not* in the repository: 11,181 vertices, 32 bones, 165 KB gzip). The engine's pose (`rig.ts`: springs, two-bone legs, a ten-joint tail, a jaw, ears and eyelids) is fitted to the model by `render/gl/fit.ts` and skinned on the graphics card. The coat is the colours its maker painted into the vertices (a pale ragdoll with grey points) lit with how open to the room each point is; the other four coats are painted over the light and dark of it by the fragment shader (tabby stripes, tuxedo and calico patches), and the eyes (which blink and look about), the nose and the mouth are drawn by the shader too. Needs WebGL1 and enough uniform vectors; otherwise, or if the mesh does not come within 8 s, or the context is lost over and over, the simple cat is drawn. (A lost context alone is not that: a phone loses it whenever the page has been in the background, and the cat is made again at once from the mesh that was fetched, up to three times in a minute with a second between tries; only a card that keeps doing it is given up on, with the reason `lost`. "Try again" starts the count afresh.)
- **Ginger cat / simple cat** (Canvas 2D): the same skeleton drawn with paths, a cartoon in outline, about a tenth of the cost. Since round 3 it is a cat in its own right, the second choice in Settings (the owner liked it and asked to keep it), and it is also the fallback of the ragdoll: when the ragdoll was chosen and the screen cannot afford it, the same painter draws it in the ragdoll's colours (white and grey), so the choice is kept. The owner has asked for sprites of the detailed cat to take that place (see "Next").
- **What is really drawn** is reported by the host (`setCatShown` in `settings.ts`: `art` = solid / classic, `why` = slow / graphics / file / lost) and read by the settings page (`useCatShown`), which says so when the ragdoll was chosen and the simple version is on the screen, and offers "try again" (`retry` on the bus: the governor and the mesh load start afresh). This is why a phone that chose the new cat and showed the old one (the owner's report) is not left looking like a choice that did nothing.
- **Governor** (`host/governor.ts`): from the *measured* cost of a frame (the cat's JavaScript plus a one-pixel read-back that makes the browser wait for the card) it moves detailed → low (half the frame rate and one canvas pixel to a screen pixel) → simple cat → no cat, and gives nothing back for a long stretch. Budget 2 ms (a desktop), 4 ms (a TV's user agent).

## Finding a title (the journey)

"Find me something" picks the title (`host/assistant.ts`) and shows the card (`host/panel.tsx`); what the cat does then is the engine's, in this order:

1. **The page makes room.** The panel scrolls the library so that the poster is whole (`bringIntoView`, `host/effects.ts`), and, when the page's fixed furniture (the hero's text, the cat's own panel, the row labels) would leave the poster's shelf without a place to stand beside it, a little further, so that the shelf has one (`shelfRoom` in `host/index.ts`: the shelf must be at least one body length and a tenth below the lowest fixed zone; the poster must stay whole).
2. **The ask waits for the cat.** 650 ms later the host sends `cat.enable` + `cat.point(id)`. When the cat is mid-leap, or its shelf has just been scrolled off, the engine keeps the ask (`pendingPoint`, for up to 8 s) and starts the trip in the first frame the cat can set out, instead of dropping to a bare point from wherever it stands (this was the bug that made the journey vanish on the real page). The host sends it once more after 1.8 s if the cat shows no sign of having taken it.
3. **The trip.** `pointAt` looks for a place on the poster's own shelf where the paw lands on the poster (`swatSpots`: a poster lies below the feet line, and the paw's reach is `SWAT_REACH`, 0.62 body lengths), and if there is one plans `swatTrip`: hops or runs there (a leap to the shelf if it is on another), creeps up the last stretch (`CREEP`), looks at the poster, wiggles, swats twice (the first after `SWAT_FIRST`, the second after `SWAT_SECOND`), points, and sits. When the shelf has no such place (the furniture of the page leaves none), it comes to the nearest legal place (`nearTrip`) and only points, rather than swatting at the air.
4. **The poster gives.** The engine's `swat` clip has a contact moment (`SWAT_HIT`, 0.44 of the swing); the host sees the clip cross it and rocks the poster (`swatPoster`, `host/effects.ts`: a small shove away from the paw and a turn, with the Web Animations API, 560 and 640 ms; nothing at all for a viewer who wants less motion, and the poster is never altered after).
5. **The cat looks back at the viewer** (a short glance) from where it sits by the poster.

Nothing is ever played or opened by the cat.

The same swat is what the cat does of its own accord when it pats a poster on its shelf.

## When the page moves under the cat

- **A row scrolled sideways** (`rideRow`): described in the table above. The cat is carried by the posters that slide (the middle of how far those still on the shelf moved, so that a row that was made over is not taken for a scroll), eased in over a few frames, and hops back or away once carried `CARRIED_FAR` (1.2 body lengths), at most every `CARRIED_EVERY` (1.4 s). It never turns away from a poster it has been sent to (a pending point, a trip or a swat in the queue).
- **A page scrolled up or down** (`adoptShelfUnder`): when the shelf the cat stood on has left the page and another shelf is where its feet are (within a quarter of a body length: it is set on the new shelf in a frame, and that is kept under what its fastest movement covers in a frame at 60 fps), the cat is on that shelf; it does not leap off.
- **A window made smaller under a walking cat** can leave the place it was walking to off the screen: the walk's goal is held to the shelf, and a cat that is more than 0.4 body lengths outside its shelf runs back in (`hurry` in `walk`) instead of strolling, cut by the edge of the screen all the way (the long soak found 2.0 s of that).
- **A page that is flung, or scrolled too far to be a step** (`measureScroll`, `rising`, `shakeOff`, `freeFall` in `engine.ts`): the engine works the page's speed out between two readings of it (`World.readAt` says when it was read; two readings less than `SCROLL_SPAN` = 12 ms apart are too near to tell a speed from) and shakes the cat off whatever it was doing on its shelf (a leap that has not landed included: the first look at a flung page drops the shelf the cat was about to leap from, and the leap's crouch used to be left hanging on it) by either of two rules. **By speed**: `SHAKE_READINGS` (2) readings in a row of more than `SHAKE_SPEED` (40) body lengths a second (a fling, a page key, a key held down); told in the page's own row pitch (`40 / 3` pitches a second), so that the size of the cat does not change the verdict. **By distance**: speed alone cannot tell a step of the remote (35–48 body lengths a second for a frame or two, at 1280 × 720) from a wheel spun or a scrollbar dragged (23–40, but for half a second or more), and a cat that rode the second kind floated up with its shelf for three or four body lengths before anything shook it off. What does tell them apart is how far the shelves go: a step of the remote is one *pitch* (the greatest gap between two neighbouring shelves on the page, 2 to 6 body lengths, 3 where it cannot be read), and two of them are two. The cat is shaken off when the shelves go up by `RISE_STEPS` (1.25) pitches inside `RISE_WINDOW` (0.5 s), in at least two readings, and at more than `RISE_SPEED` (12) body lengths a second over the last `RISE_SPAN` (0.1 s). The first movement of a page that had not moved for half a second does not count when it is a single jump of at least `JUMP_SHARE` (0.4) of a pitch: the first step of the remote from the top of the library folds the banner (a jump of 1.5 body lengths) and then scrolls a step, and a notch of the wheel is an instant jump of 1.35; a first step and a first notch ride with the shelf. A cat that is shaken off falls under gravity where it is, drawn with its legs reaching down (`FREE_FALL_POSE`), and is handed to a landing (the first place under it, as a cat that is let go of lands) when the page has slowed to `CATCH_SPEED` (8; read over the last `CATCH_SPAN` = 0.1 s, so that the pause between two steps does not count as a page that has stopped), when it is within `HANDOVER` (2.2) body lengths of the foot of the screen (which it then lands on), or after `FREE_FALL_MAX` (3 s). A page that has not been read for `SCROLL_STILL` (0.15 s) is still. A page that is already scrolled up (the shelves going down) only has the speed rule. Not shaken: a quiet cat or one for a viewer who wants less motion (it only rides), a cat in a hand, already falling, fading or about to come back somewhere else, a cat on a trip to a poster it was asked to show, a cat in a film, and a cat on the foot of the screen (which does not go with the page).
- **A page that is read now and then is carried on** (`host/carry.ts`, `TvPage.read`): a read of the page is dear on a slow screen (the host keeps it to a tenth of the time), so a cat that only knew the last read would be left behind by its shelf in steps of every third or fifth frame. Between reads the host asks where the page's scroller (`.tv-rows`, or `.tv-grid` in a category, a list or the results of a search) has been scrolled to, and hands the engine the last read moved by the difference: shelves, the names under the cards, the cards and the cards' keep-outs that are on the scroller, and the card with the focus; what is fixed (the top bar, the hero, the foot of the screen, the cat's own panel) stays. The engine is given a new world at every frame of a scroll, as on a fast screen, and the carried page is the page a read at that frame would have given, to the pixel (`carry.test.ts`). In a browser with the processor slowed four times, the cat's feet were up to 1.3 body lengths from its shelf in steps; they are now on the shelf at every frame (0 px, 0 stalls).
- **The thirty-picture screen** (`follow` in `host/index.ts`): the governor's lower picture is drawn every other frame, and the loop used to do nothing at all in between, so the cat's place moved at thirty a second while the rows went by at sixty. In the frames in between the brain now runs and the picture is moved when the page has moved (a carried page, or one read again); the skeleton and the painting wait for their frame and are told how long it was (`owed`). A cat in a hand, and a page that has not moved, cost nothing there.
- Anything else that takes the shelf away (a menu, a smaller window) is the older rule: leap to another shelf, or fade out and come back somewhere else.

## Reaching and climbing

The `climb` clip (`feat/cat-5-moods`): the cat takes hold of the top edge of a shelf by its forepaws, hangs, and hauls itself up over it.

- **From a hand** (`dropTarget` with `reach`, `reachableLip`, `letGo`, `reachFor` in `engine.ts`): the cat that is let go of near a lip does not need to be above it. A ledge whose line is above the cat's feet by more than it can spring (`SPRING` 0.6 body lengths) and by no more than `CLIMB_UP` (1.65), and no further across than `CLIMB_ACROSS` (1.2: it twists in the air at the rate a fall steers), is a place it can come to, at a price (`CLIMB_COST` 0.85 for every body length up, `CLIMB_ACROSS_COST` 1.2 for every one across, and `CLIMB_FIXED` 0.2 for the show) set against the plain fall to what is under it: the nearer of the two wins, so a lip a hand's breadth out of reach is climbed and a fall of a body length is a fall. Its hanging place (a box from the line of the lip down `CLIMB_HANG`, 0.7 body lengths outside the lip and 0.35 inside) has to be on the screen and clear of the keep-out zones, and the floor and a film's perch are never climbed to. It faces the inside of the ledge (the way it faces now when that is the same to it) and the lip is `CLIMB_LIP` (0.6) behind the place it ends up standing at. The reach takes as long as the rise or the fall to where its paws are at the lip (at least 0.2 s), steering across as it goes; if the page takes the lip away meanwhile (a scroll), it falls to what is under it.
- **A low step up in a route** (`leapBetween` in `ledges.ts`, `routeTo` in `engine.ts`): a cat in no hurry that has to go up a step of `STEP_MIN` to `STEP_MAX` (1.2 to 1.6 body lengths: the real library's foot of the screen to the third row is 1.34) walks to the place under the lip, `CLIMB_BACK` (0.95) behind where it will stand, and climbs, instead of leaping at it; a cat that is called, or runs, still leaps.
- **The clip** (`CLIMB_TIME` 1.7 s, `progress` 0..1, marks `CLIMB_GRAB` 0.20 = it has hold, `CLIMB_PULL` 0.60 = the chest is over the lip and the paws are on the ledge, `CLIMB_OVER` 0.85 = the hind legs are over): the frame's `x`,`y` are held at the point the cat ends up standing at (the feet on the line of the ledge) all the way, `facing` is the way along the ledge, and `grounded` is false until the last frame (which is `progress` 1, the cat on its feet; the frame after is the next thing it does: it sits, and washes 70 % of the time). The picture starts with the cat hanging from the lip **below that point** (its toes `CLIMB_HANG`, 1.4 body lengths, under the line, its middle `CLIMB_BACK` behind the point) and the art does the vertical travel; the engine moves nothing in y during the climb. The simple cat draws it (`render/climb.ts`, `Solved.shift`, the rig's climb scene); the solid cat (`render/gl/`) has no clip for it yet and falls back to its jump (see the director's `PLANS` in the report of this branch).
- A cat that hangs or climbs is not stroked, called, told or picked up (the host's hit box is off for it), a menu or a hidden tab takes it away at once like any leap, and a hand's grab is ignored until it is up.
- `lab`: `node scripts/cat/sheet.mjs climb <out.png> --scale=2` draws the reach and the climb from the air, `climb-walk` from under the lip.
- **Up the face of a card** (`scale`, `feat/cat-scale-engine`): a step too high for a hang (over `STEP_MAX`, up to `SCALE_MAX` 4.5 body lengths) can be climbed straight up the face of the card, seen from behind, the side climb above then hauls the cat over the lip. `World.rearView` gates it: the host sets it only when the brush can draw the `scale` clip (the solid cat), and without it the engine plans and moves exactly as before. In a route the rises above what can be leapt become scale hops, and leapable ones over `STEP_MAX` are scaled half the time (the engine's seeded generator); calm cats only (no run, no reduced motion), never up a face that a keep-out zone covers. The act: from under the lip, `x` fixed, facing right, `grounded` false, up at 0.6 body lengths a second after a 0.35 s ease-in, the odometer plays the clip (its stride is the distance climbed), no look; 0.8 body lengths under the lip it becomes the side climb with no fade. Busy like the climb (no grab, stroke, call or command, the host's hand box is off); a ledge that goes or moves on the way up, or a painter that is lost, lets it fall to what is under it. The clip is `scale` in `cat.clips` (`docs/cat-animation`).

## Held by the scruff

- **The hang** (`rig.ts`): the cat hangs from the hand at the nape (`HANG_DOWN` 0.73 body lengths above its feet, `HANG_AHEAD` 0.1 behind them, both in `types.ts` now, the host's hand model reads the same numbers), the body slung from there at a pitch of -1.2 rad with a curl at the waist (`HANG_CURL`), the hind legs and the tail hanging, the forelegs tucked, and it sways slowly about the hand (two slow sines, 0.42 and 0.97 Hz) instead of swinging like a pendulum. The sway is a turn **about the grip**, not about the middle of the body (a pivot at the middle made the hand's hold slide along the cat): the picture is turned about the middle and moved by the difference, which the simple painter applies through `Solved.shift` (the solid cat ignores `shift`, as it does for the climb, and so turns about its middle).
- **The fight** (`CatFrame.struggle`, 0..1: 0 calm, about 0.1 limp, 1 all-out; `wrestle` in `engine.ts`; the numbers are the temperament's and `FIGHT_*`): calm for `heldCalm` (2.2 s), then a bout builds up over `FIGHT_BUILD` (1.3 s) to a peak of `heldStrength` (0.85, times 0.9 to 1), is kept up for `heldStamina` (3 s, times 0.7 to 1.3), goes limp for 1.4 to 2.6 s and tries again, `FIGHT_TIRE` (0.92) as hard as the last time (these are the cat as it was; the ginger and the ragdoll have their own, see Characters). The number follows that at `FIGHT_FOLLOW` (3.5 a second) and is gone half a second after the hand lets go (a cat that is let go of in the air is not still fighting on the shelf: the soak checks for it). A cat in a hand that is not moved fights just the same, and a stroke (a press that only wobbles) does not lift it, so it does not fight.
- **How it looks** (`rig.ts`, from `struggle` alone, so the solid cat gets it too): the body twists about the hand and bends at the waist, the hind legs kick (out of step, not both at once), a forepaw swipes at the hand, the ears go flat, the mouth opens, the tail whips; all of it comes in with the number, through a spring (4 Hz: when `struggle` jumps from 0 to 1 and back, no joint of the cat moves more than 0.23 body lengths in a frame at 60 a second, and the test allows 0.3). `Emote` has two new values for the same family of feelings: `annoyed` (ears flat back, eyes narrowed, tail lashing) and `shake` (the body shaken from head to tail, about a second).
- **Landing annoyed** (`annoyedLanding`): a cat that was fighting at least `UPSET_AT` (0.3) when the hand let go, and lands within `UPSET_FOR` (8 s), does not sit and wash by the book (`afterLanding`). Its temperament weights four things: `huff` (stands, ears back and tail lashing, back to where the hand was, and walks 2.2 to 3.4 body lengths along the shelf away from it, towards the side with room), `groom` (sits and washes, as if it had meant to be there), `startle` (a fright, a look at the hand, and a run), `shake` (shakes itself, sits, may wash). The weights now are the cat as it has always been (`BASELINE` in `engine/temperament.ts`): 0.4, 0.3, 0.1, 0.2. The choice and the fight's timings use their own random stream (`temper`), so a cat that is never picked up chooses everything else exactly as before (the behaviour table of 8 cats × 300 s is the same, character by character, before and after).
- `lab`: `node scripts/cat/sheet.mjs struggle <out.png> --scale=2` (and `struggle-left` for the cat facing the other way) draws the held cat at twelve moments of a hold, calm, building, all-out, limp, and the next bout, with a red cross where the hand is; `feelings` the annoyed cat and the shaking one beside a calm one and a startled one.

## Going about

The cat that is free to roam (not quiet, not asked for less motion: those two are exactly as they were) goes about the library more, and is not left at the foot of the screen (`choose` in `engine.ts`; the numbers are the constants after `PLAY_REACH`).

- **The foot of the screen is a place to pass through.** On a page that has shelves, a cat down on the foot is "low": everything else it might do is worth `FOOT_STAY` (0.3) of what it is worth anywhere else, it does not nap there, and a hop up onto a shelf is worth `FOOT_UP` (2.4, against about 1 for a rest). Of its own accord it goes down there only `FOOT_WEIGHT` (0.25) as readily as the page's weight for the place says (a hop, coming in, the landings of the zoomies: `hopAround` leaves the foot out when there are shelves). A page that has nothing but the foot is not "low" (it is the cat's whole world: it naps and wanders there as before), and nor is a cat that cannot roam. The same, milder, for a shelf whose names leave no place to sit clear of them when another shelf has one (`crowded`: `CROWDED_UP` 1.8, `CROWDED_STAY` 0.5), and a stop where its body would hide a name is cut to `STOP_SHORT` (0.35) of its length; a hop prefers the shelf where it can settle clear of the names.
- **Livelier.** Wandering, hopping, stalking a poster, looking over a lip at one, patting one and the zoomies are worth more (and come round again sooner: the cooldowns of prowl, inspect, pat and the zoomies are about two thirds of what they were), a rest is 3 to 9 seconds, a nap 20 to 50 and only after 50 seconds of nobody about. Its energy is not a flat 0.32 but swings 0.2 either side of that in a cycle of `LIVELY_EVERY` (210 s) that each cat begins at a place of its own (the seed), and what goes about is worth more at the top of the swing: a cat has its busy spells and its quiet ones.
- **It accompanies the viewer.** `follow`: a card that got the remote's focus within `FOLLOW_WITHIN` (14 s), on a shelf the cat is not on: it goes over, sits by the card and looks at it (a viewer going down the library gets the cat along, a shelf behind). `greet`: a pointer that has rested for `GREET_STILL` (1.2 s) more than `GREET_NEAR` (2.2) body lengths off: it comes to the shelf under it, a paw's length under the pointer if there is one, sits looking at it, and may bat at it then (a pointer nearer than that is the bat's). `visit`: now and then a poster on another shelf is gone to, crept up on and patted, and the cat sits. The posters are found by name, and the same film can be on two shelves: a pat or a point goes to the one nearest where the cat set out for (`posterOf`).
- **Numbers**, 40 cats × 15 minutes (`scripts/cat/behaviour.mts`), the cat before this one → this:

  |---|---|---|---|
  | on the foot of the screen | 13.9 % → 1.0 % | 24.3 % → 2.1 % | 18.7 % → 10.4 % |
  | a stay on the foot, on average | 48 s → 7 s | 94 s → 11 s | |
  | hops a minute | 0.92 → 1.66 | 0.77 → 1.46 | 5.5 → 5.9 |
  | walking about | 13.6 % → 18.9 % | 14.1 % → 19.8 % | |

  Called down to the foot, a cat is on a shelf again after a median of 11.7 s (it was 29 s; a tenth of them took more than a minute, and now and then three; now the slowest of 40 took 32 s). In the real page in Chromium (`scripts/cat/browser-lab.mjs places`, 12 cats × 4 minutes, and `floor`, 5 cats): alone, 29.1 % → 3.0 % of the time on the foot (the longest stay 188 s → 21 s); with a viewer pressing the remote's arrows, 37.6 % → 8.6 % (the rest of it is what the page does to it: a shelf scrolled off); called down, on a shelf again after 15.7 s on average (it was 50.5). Hops a minute alone 0.58 → 1.00, time asleep 31 % → 19 %, time walking 12 % → 17 %.
- Not changed: a quiet cat, a cat for a viewer who wants less motion, a cat in a film, a cat in a hand, and the whole of the cat's reactions to the page (the fall, the ride, the reach, the fight).

## Characters

The two cats the settings offer are two characters (`feat/cat-5-moods`): the ginger is friendly, curious and restless; the white and grey ragdoll is grumpy and lazy. A character is a table of named numbers (`Temperament` in `engine/temperament.ts`) that the engine's choices read; no rule asks which cat it is. `BASELINE` is the cat as it was before there were two, and the one every test and tool starts from: it is the same cat frame for frame (checked against the commit before the characters: 12 cats × 10 minutes alone on the TV's library, and 12 whole bad afternoons). The host picks the table by the coat (`temperamentOf(coat)` in `host/index.ts`: `ginger`, `ragdoll`, and any other coat, `?catcoat=calico` say, is the baseline) when the engine is made and whenever the settings change the coat. `CatEngine.setTemperament` moves the cat's energy and curiosity by the change in the table's anchors, so a cat swapped in the settings is livelier or lazier at once, not a little at a time.

## Round 5: every word, every page, over the film's controls, and a menu

- **Every word of a page.** The page reader no longer lists the parts of the library it knew by name (the heading of a row, the caption of a card) and misses the rest. `host/text.ts` reads what the browser draws: every line of text, and every button, link, switch and field, as laid out; `host/lines.ts` puts the pieces into lines and finds the places in front of a page's first words (and, on a grid of cards, the foot of a card's picture above its name, a ledge of its own where the tops of the cards are cut to nothing). The words are zones the cat keeps out of; on the library's rows the captions stay words to settle away *from* (hard captions took the library's ledges away). Measured from the page itself while the cat played about: before, 37–100 % of its time on its feet was spent over words on the categories page, a category, a list and a search; after, none (`covered` in the debug snapshot says how much of the page's words the cat covers, `readMs` what a read costs). The letter that stands for a title without a picture is art, not words.
- **Every page of the TV.** The cat's layer is a child of the TV's box, over any page of it, and the reader reads any page the way it reads the library: the places to stand are the tops of the boxes the page draws that are wide enough and in sight, plus the lines in front of its first words, plus the foot of the screen; pictures that say something (a code to scan) are boxes to keep out of; two buttons side by side are two boxes, not one line; a page taller than the screen scrolls and what is on its scroller goes with it. Kept away as before: a genuine modal (a film's menu, the party panel, the feedback form, the first-run welcome), a page laid over a film, and the pages that only say something is on its way.
- **A layer over the film's controls.** `.kino-cat[data-mode="player"]` is at `z-index` 2 inside the TV's box: over the controls' bars (1) and the pause shade, under the subtitles (`.tv > .tv-captions`, raised to 3) and under what the player puts up in passing (12 and above). In the library it is 65, its menu 68, its panel 70. The box that takes a hand is the cat's body (the same box as in the library) and nothing more, and what it takes is only the *start* of a press (`pointerdown`, `mousedown`, `click`, `dblclick`, `auxclick` and `touchstart` are stopped on it): the move and the release are never held back, because the player's gestures and the seek bar's drag end on a release anywhere on the page (a scrub let go of over the cat still ends: e2e). `host/perch.ts`: the perch is the stretch of the controls' row with no button in it; where the row leaves none (a narrow screen, a row that has wrapped) it is a corner of the picture on the line where the bars' lowest block begins (`BARS_ABOVE`, never higher than half the screen), so it is over no button there either. The engine had to let a cat in a film be carried: one line in `canBeCarried` (`engine.ts`) and its test.
- **The menu and the sleep.** `host/menu.ts` (the card; a plain `div` of buttons appended beside the cat, with its own listeners that are all taken off when it closes) and, in `host/index.ts`, `openMenu` (a right click's `contextmenu`, or a finger that rests 0.55 s without moving: not on a cat that is being carried, asleep, or out of reach), `sendToSleep` and `wake`. The sleep is the host's and not the brain's: the brain's "away" is a cat that a *page* has sent off, and it comes back with the next page that has a place for it. `asleep` keeps the cat out of sight (`data-asleep` = `curl` or `fade` on the layer; `cat.css` plays the fade) while the page is still read and the brain still runs a few times a second, so that it is where the page has a place for it when it is woken. Waking: `wake()` from the panel (`useCatAsleep` in `settings.ts` turns its screen into "The cat is asleep." + "Wake the cat") or from the bus (`emitCat({ type: "wake" })`, the row of the cat's page in Settings, `tv/Account.tsx`). `?cat=debug` adds `menu` and `asleep` to the snapshot. The card closes at the first thing that is not for it (Back, a press elsewhere, another key, the window changing) and at the page going by under it: a `scroll` (heard in the capture phase) of anything *inside* the TV's box. A scroll of that box itself, or of the document, is left alone, because the card is in that box with the cat's layer and goes with it; and that box does have a few pixels to scroll in a film once the controls have slid away (the bar sticks out of it by about 18 px at 1280 wide), which is how a robot's retried click (`scrollIntoView({ block: "start" })`) once closed the card under its own click. The card comes in with a fade and does not slide, so nothing aims at a card that is still moving.

## Code map

- `src/client/cat/engine/`: the brain, no browser: `engine.ts` (acts, moods, choice, leaps; `measureScroll` / `rising` / `shakeOff` / `freeFall`: a page that is flung or scrolled too far), `temperament.ts` (the table of numbers that says what sort of cat it is), `ledges.ts` (where it can stand; leap planning; where to settle), `sim.ts` (a made-up page and bad afternoons for tests, page flings among them).
- `src/client/cat/render/`: `rig.ts` (springs, two-bone legs, tail, scenes), `swat.ts` (the swing of a paw at a poster, as a pure function of the clip's phase: raised, brought down through the line to the poster at `SWAT_HIT`, pressed, flicked back), `draw.ts` (the simple cat), `gl/` (skeleton, shader, coats, renderer, the mesh).
