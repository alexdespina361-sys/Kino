# Subtitle sync (the auto aligner)

Branch `feat/subtitle-sync`. The research behind it, its settings and its numbers are the lab's: `output/subtitle-lab/subtitle-sync-kit/` and its updates (git-excluded). The kit's aligner is vendored **unchanged** in `src/client/subsync/vendor/subtitle-sync.js` (the kit's update 2, `web/subtitle-sync.mjs`: sha256 `0f57a7d857a22cd22aa62f07a5b1ce113047c459572f324aa1416d7ba8fdf122`, kit `VERSION` "2026-10-17.3"); everything else here is glue that feeds it sound as the player downloads it and puts its answer on the subtitle lines.

## What the viewer gets

- **On by default, nothing to do.** When a film that plays through hls.js shows a subtitle, the lines are moved to when they are said, at least as fast as the film plays. A switch called "Auto-sync" in the TV menu (Adjust column) and in the phone's subtitles sheet turns it off; the choice is kept (`subtitleSync` in `tv.prefs`) and turning it off puts every line back where the file had it (also after the aligner gave up on its own). The manual Delay stays on top of whatever the aligner does.
- **Right subtitles, picked for them.** The first pick is used. That is the best-fitting release of the language (WEB, then BluRay, then DVD, then the rest, never a track of only songs or forced lines: `releaseRank` in `src/shared/tracks.ts`), unless the viewer's remembered label is there. Once enough is heard, the words said are compared with the track; if most are not in it (another film's file, a release for another cut), the other English tracks of the same film are tried the same way, in that order of release, and a clearly better one replaces it between two lines. The viewer's remembered language/label is not touched, and a track the viewer picks themselves ends the auto-pick for that film. A track the provider gave no language for is never taken for English (it would be weighed against English speech and replaced), so it is never switched away from either.
- **Any language.** The words can only be heard in English (Whistle, see below). A track in another language is paired line for line with an English track of the same film that has been found right and takes its times (the dub is the same, so the cuts are the same); lines the aligner has not reached yet take that track's own shift, not the English file's. No choice to make.
- **English dubs.** When the sound is English and no English track of the film has the words that are said (the subtitles translate the Japanese of a film that is watched in its English dub: Dragon Ball Z: Bojack Unbound), the track on screen stays and its lines are timed as a dub (below), not left several seconds late as the voice detector alone leaves them. A track that does have the words is timed exactly as before.
- **Other viewers' work is reused.** What one screen finds right is left on the server (times only, no text, nothing personal); the next screen playing the same file on the same cut checks it against its own sound and, once it agrees, uses it. A complete entry means the 17 MB recogniser is **never downloaded** on that screen.

## How it works (`src/client/subsync/`)

| Step | File | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | `tap.ts`, `audio.ts`, `decode.ts` | The **player** owns an `AudioTap` from the moment it makes hls.js (engine, main bundle, ~1 KB): it listens to `BUFFER_APPENDING` (audio / audiovideo) and always learns the init segments (a few hundred bytes), whether or not the aligner is on, so an aligner turned on in the middle of a film can read what comes next. Pieces are copied only while someone takes them, or while the aligner's code is still on its way (the first 16 MB are kept for it). The session decodes them with an `OfflineAudioContext` at 16 kHz mono (no sound device is opened; ~4 ms per second of audio), one at a time. Plain `mp4`, native HLS (Safari) have no tap: the setting says "not available". A sound the browser cannot decode (AC-3) is found out when the first three pieces fail: the aligner gives up quietly (the chunk and the worker's weights have been fetched by then, see below). |
| Start | `session.ts`, `engine.ts` | The film does not wait for the aligner: hls.js starts at once and the aligner's code (a lazy chunk, ~20 KB gzipped) is attached when it arrives. The session then does **nothing until a subtitle track is on screen**: pieces are queued (at most 16 MB, the ones farthest from the picture go first), nothing is decoded, and the worker and the voice detector's 1.2 MB of weights are not fetched. When a track comes up, the worker starts and the queue is decoded, **the piece nearest the picture first** (what is behind it counts as four times as far), so a viewer who turns subtitles on in the middle of a film is timed from where they are, not from the oldest sound that was kept; when subtitles are turned off the decoding pauses again. |
| Voice detector | `silero.ts`, `backend.ts`, `subsync.worker.ts` | Silero VAD v5 as a 200-line JavaScript port (1.2 MB of weights instead of onnxruntime's 14 MB wasm; it matches onnxruntime to 2e-6). Runs in a worker on everything that is decoded. A device that spends more than 80 % of real time on it gives up quietly. |
| Words | `whistle.ts`, `schedule.ts` | Whistle (cactus-needle, 7 languages, wasm, its own module worker) listens to 15 s windows, nearest the picture first, only windows it can finish before the picture gets there, at a duty cycle of 30–80 % of its time (less on devices with 2 cores). A slow device hears every other window or fewer; the aligner places the lines in between from their neighbours. |
| Solve | `solve.ts`, `core.ts` | The kit's `align()` is made for a whole clip. Here it runs on windows ending at the newest sound (60 s with words, 150 s voice only; the first 10 s are left to the solve before, the last 2 s to the next), and a window whose lines move too differently from each other (10th to 90th percentile more than 20 s apart) is dropped. Words heard and ≥ 30 % of the lines matched: placed with the words (`whistle+silero`). Words heard, fewer matched, and ≥ 0.25 sure words (probability ≥ 0.5) per second of sound listened to: placed as a dub (`dub`, see Dub mode). Otherwise the voice detector alone (`silero`). |
| Apply | `apply.ts`, `cues.ts` | The times are written on the page's own `TextTrackCue`s (so `Captions.tsx` needs nothing), the originals are kept, and a line on screen is moved only once it is off. Lines no solve has reached take the track's shift so far, so a seek lands near. What was timed while the track was not on screen (subtitles were off for a while, or it was the English track another was timed from) is put in place when the track comes up. |
| Choose | `pick.ts`, `hold.ts`, `session.ts` | Described above. Needs ≥ 200 words over ≥ 120 s; share of words found in matching lines ≥ 0.25 = right, < 0.08 = wrong, between = look again when 2.5 × the words are in (at most 4 looks). The test looks at the first minutes that were heard; if they hold too few words and the recogniser has been through more than they are (a quiet opening, a device that hears only some windows), it looks at the newest words instead, and does not ask again until more has been heard. A track the viewer chose is tested but never replaced (no other track is loaded for it). A "wrong" verdict with no clearly right track to switch to, while English is heard at ≥ 0.25 sure words per second, is a dub (Dub mode): the track stays, the verdict reads "dub", the film's tracks are not tested again until it has more English ones, and the recogniser is not given up on. A candidate is loaded hidden and held back from `Captions`; when it replaces the shown track it is timed from the sound already heard, within a few seconds. |
| Other languages | `bridge.ts` | Pairs a track with an English one by line times (also when one runs 4.2 % faster or slower, the 23.976/25 fps case), then follows it line for line. |
| Share | `share.ts`, `src/shared/subsync.ts`, `src/server/subsync/` | Below. |
| Wiring | `start.ts`, `engine.ts` | `startSync` is a lazily loaded chunk; the engine passes the video, the hls.js instance, the tap and `SET_SUBTITLE_SYNC`. `PlayerState.subtitleSync` is `off / ready / syncing / synced / unavailable` (`ready` = on, nothing to time yet). |

**Safety.** Nothing is downloaded (beyond the ~20 KB chunk) until a subtitle is showing and the browser has a worker and `OfflineAudioContext`. The recogniser is skipped when `deviceMemory` < 2, or when the page died while loading it three times in a row (a mark in `localStorage` set before the load and cleared after the first window: `guard.ts`; retried after 3 days). A failure anywhere = status `unavailable` and the subtitles stay as they came (what was already timed stays too, until the viewer turns the aligner off). Turning the aligner off while it starts is not a failure.

**A changed audio track** (a dub) throws away what was heard and the timings that were asked for or are to be left: another sound is another timing. The same track again (hls.js says so when it moves to a variant of the film that has its audio in another group) does not: it is told by language and name, and the init segments are kept either way.

## Dub mode (the kit's update 2)

For English subtitles over English speech whose words are not the words that are said: the film is watched in its English dub and both subtitle files translate the original (Dragon Ball Z: Bojack Unbound). The file was made for the same picture, so its lines are in the right order and about the right places, and the voice detector alone, which follows the wrong speech there, leaves them seconds late. The kit's `AsrAnchor.dub` moves the file's timing by one offset (up to 10 s, only when clearly more lines then fall on a heard phrase than with no offset), puts each line on the start of the phrase that is heard near it (within 0.6 s, waiting up to 2.5 s) and keeps the lines in their order and not on screen together.

- **When a window is a dub** (`solve.ts`): words were heard, fewer than 30 % of the lines matched them, and ≥ 0.25 sure words per second were heard (`heardRate` in `pick.ts`; the kit's own line, so a sound the recogniser cannot read, with few or unsure words, stays with the voice detector alone). A track in another language never uses words, so never a dub. The method is `dub`.
- **Which solves look at the long window.** Once a solve calls the track a dub, the ones after it use the 150 s window until the heard words place a window again. Not in the hand-off, and its own commit (`git revert` it to go back to the minute): the dub learns its offset from how many lines fall on a phrase, a minute holds 5–17 lines in the first minutes of a film, and a wrong offset learned there stays (the solver keeps the shift it learned). Numbers below.
- **When the track is a dub** (`session.ts`): the right/wrong test finds "wrong", no other English track is clearly right (so none is switched to), and ≥ 0.25 sure words per second were heard. The shown track stays, the verdict reads "dub", the film's other English tracks are not tested again until it has more of them (more sound does not make them better), and the recogniser is not given up on (two "wrong" rounds used to stop it, which would have stopped dub mode). A right English track that shows up still replaces a wrong one. A track the viewer chose themselves is treated the same.
- **Held like the voice detector alone.** A dub is the file's timing put on phrases, not what the film says, so nothing of it is left on the server (a dub is never "verified", and only verified work is shared); `absorb()` counts its shifts as the voice detector's.
- **To see it** (`?debug`, the aligner line): `solves 9 (words 2, dub 6, voice 1)` and `tests 1: dub 0.05`. The first is how the solves placed the lines (a film that is a dub shows `dub` rising), the second the share of the heard words that the shown track has (a dub's is near 0).
- **Not built, on purpose:** subtitles made from the speech ("English (auto-generated)", a switch to one). The kit's `AsrAnchor.generate` is in the vendored file and nothing calls it.
- **Limits.** Measured on the lab's recorded signals of one film (below), not by playing a film through the app in a browser. A dub whose lines say what is said in other words and share names with the speech may let the shift probe (the one that finds a track that is a minute off from the words that do match) find a wrong offset from those names: not seen on the one recording, not tried on others. The first minute or so of a film is timed from few lines, as it is for any track. A dub of a film with another number of cuts (another version of the picture) is only as good as the file's cuts.
- **What it changes for a track that is another film's.** When no other English track fits, such a track is now handled like a dub's: the recogniser is not stopped and the lines are put on the nearest heard phrases (before: after two "wrong" tests, which a long film reaches, the recogniser was stopped and the voice detector alone placed the lines). The words of the file are as wrong as ever.

## Sharing what was aligned

- **Name of an entry:** a 128-bit hash of `kit VERSION | asset version | audio language | the file's lines (times and words without markup) | the film's length to the second`. The nearest other second is asked for too (a length can fall either side of the edge); a screen leaves its work under the name it found an entry under, else the first. A source whose film starts a few seconds later (a logo) is a film that is a few seconds longer, and its lines are said at other times: it must not share a timing with the others, because a timing that is a few seconds off cannot be told from a right one by the voice detector (below). A new kit or model never mixes with old entries.
- **Entry:** `{v: 1, lines, placed: [[line, startMoved, endMoved, words 0|1], ...]}`, moves in centiseconds (within ±300 s). No text, no viewer, no address is stored. 15–40 KB for a film.
- **Server** (`GET/PUT /api/subsync/:key`): memory, a folder (`DATA_DIR/subsync`) or the Postgres table `kino_subsync`, like the accounts. 1000 entries at most (least recently used out; about 30 MB in practice, 200 MB at the very most: a free Postgres is 500 MB and is shared with the accounts). A PUT is merged line by line: placements with words beat those without, newer beats older, an unchanged entry is not written, another number of lines is another file (409). A GET for a file nobody has timed yet is answered 204, not 404: it is the usual answer, and a browser prints every 404 in its console as an error. Merges of one key are done one at a time. Reads 120/min and writes 60 per 10 min per address, bodies ≤ 192 KiB, 8000 lines, schema-checked (`zod`). `/api/health` shows `subsync: {found, missed, stored}`. **Behind a proxy that is not Render, set `TRUST_PROXY=1`**, or every viewer shares one address and these limits are for the whole site.
- **Never believed on the server's word.** A screen asks once the length is known and **changes nothing** until its own solves agree with the entry: a stretch of ≥ 8 common lines with ≥ 60 % within 4 s is good (the voice detector alone, which is all there is at that point, is a second or three off the words on real sound; what is to be caught is another cut or another file, which is further off than that); two good stretches make the entry believed, three bad ones drop it. The recogniser waits for the answer (and for the check, at most 45 s) and is not fetched at all if a believed entry places ≥ 90 % of the lines. For a partial entry it is not fetched until there is something left to listen to, and its windows skip what the entry covers. A believed entry also skips the right/wrong test (it passed once). Its lines (with words or without) take the place of what the voice detector alone had placed, and are not made worse by it afterwards, because on real sound it is a second or three off all through a stretch and now and then four or five on a line (measured on the lab clip: against an entry that was right to 0.2 s, the lines of a stretch were 1.4 to 3.7 s early, the middle of them 2.4 s, and one line 4.9 s). The entry is let go of **by the stretch** where this screen's own solve covers lines that are mostly (≥ 40 %) further than 4 s from the entry's (another cut further on), never a line at a time.
- **What a screen leaves:** only a track known to be this film's (English verdict "right", a bridged track, a track the aligner switched to, or an entry it believed), only lines it placed itself, only what it has not left before, every 3 minutes (≥ 10 new lines) and a last time as the page closes (`keepalive`, ≥ 3 lines, the first 1500). A failed upload is tried again; it counts as left only on 204. An upload the server refuses for good (409 another file, 400, 413) is not sent again for that track.
- **Known limits.** There are no accounts on this and a key is computable by anyone who has the subtitle file, so a vandal can PUT bad timings, or a wrong number of lines under a real key (every honest upload for it is then refused until that entry is evicted, and screens ignore it). The check above rejects an entry that is wrong where this screen listens by more than 4 s, and a believed entry is overruled by the stretch where this screen hears it clearly wrong (a different cut further on is reached as the film is downloaded; lines not reached yet show the entry's times). **An entry that is off by less than 4 s everywhere is not caught**: the voice detector alone, which is all a screen has to check with (so that the recogniser's 17 MB need not be downloaded), is as far off as that itself. What keeps such entries out is the name: sources whose film is a second or two longer or shorter do not share one (see above). A vandal's uniform shift of up to 4 s, or an unlucky pair of sources with the same length and different lead-ins, shows a screen lines up to about 4 s from where they are said for the whole film. The merge keeps words over no words, so a bad entry is not corrected on the server by voice-only uploads, only by newer word-placed ones. If it ever matters: ask for two agreeing uploads from different addresses before serving an entry (that keeps a vandal's shift out as well), or put a byte budget on the table.

## Cost to the host and the device

- **First use on a device:** the chunk (~20 KB gzipped), then, once a subtitle is showing, the voice detector 1.2 MB, plus, unless a shared entry covers the file, the recogniser 17 MB + 0.9 MB + 54 KB. All from `/subtitle-sync/1/`, served `immutable` (a changed file goes in a new folder), and the model is kept by the browser in Cache Storage; a device downloads it once. With the host's bandwidth limited, this folder is the first thing to move to a CDN (`ASSET_BASE` in `assets.ts`) if the aligner is used a lot.
- Repository: the folder (19 MB, mostly the recogniser's model) is in `public/` and, built, in `dist/client/` (committed as before). The two copies are the same files, which git keeps once, so the repository grows by about 19 MB.
- **Device:** about half of one core while it works; nothing while no subtitles are on (the queue of undecoded sound is at most 16 MB, the decoded sound kept for the recogniser at most 24 MB). The voice detector gets through sound far faster than real time; the recogniser is the cost.

## What was measured

Real Chromium (Playwright) with the lab's 6-minute Boondocks clip as VP9/Opus fMP4 HLS, the real models, the real worker:

- Decoding 360 s of sound: ~1 s. Silero on 360 s: 9.4 s. Whistle ~6–8 × real time. Verdict at ~35 s of wall time (120 s of sound heard).
- A right track (3 s late): mean error against the reference 0.98 s → 0.22 s.
- A wrong first track (another film's file): found wrong (share 0.00), a right one picked and aligned: 1.02 s → 0.21 s.
- Two viewers, one after the other, on a server that kept the entry: the first uploaded about 170 lines (PUT 204, 2.8 KB) after 110 s; the second asked, found it (GET 200), believed it within 5–10 s with a third of the 511 lines placed from it, and did not need the recogniser for those; for the lines the entry did not place it listened as usual. The first 38 lines were on average 0.29 s from the reference for the second (against 1.12 s for the file and 0.22 s when it computed everything itself): the entry is a head start. (Measuring this found that the voice detector alone, which the second screen has between belief and the recogniser, put some lines of a stretch 2–5 s early; when those were allowed to replace the entry's lines the same 38 lines were 0.89 s off. The entry's lines are kept now unless the whole stretch disagrees.)
- **Dub mode, on the lab's recorded Bojack signals** (the voice detector's probabilities and the recogniser's words from the kit's own fixture, 5.5 minutes, played through the site's solver the way `solve.test.ts` plays a film: sound in pieces, a window of words at a time, a solve after each; two tracks, 18 hand-paired reference lines each; comfortable / within 0.3 s / within 1 s / off by more than 2 s): the files as downloaded 8 / 25 / 42 / 22 %, the voice detector alone (what update 1 gave) 6 / 6 / 19 / 81 %, dub mode with the short windows 36 / 56 / 69 / 17 %, with the long windows after the first dub solve 47 / 64 / 83 / 3 %, the lab's own answer 50 / 67 / 83 / 3 %. The kit's `test/check.mjs` ends with "all checks passed" (the matching subtitles give update 1's answer exactly). The short windows lose on the first minute and a half of the second track: a window of 5–17 lines learns a wrong offset there, which is why the long window follows a dub.
- The playhead of the headless test browser stalled for a minute at a time while the test socket did not say it was alive (the server drops a phone that is silent for 60 s); with the test phone pinging, the clip plays through with the aligner on or off.
- The unit tests run the whole session against a synthetic film with a fake recogniser, including the shared-timing paths; the end-to-end spec checks the plumbing in a real browser.

## Not done, on purpose

- Not run on a real TV or phone: the numbers above are one desktop Chromium, with a VP9/Opus fMP4 stream. Real streams are mostly MPEG-TS that hls.js turns into AAC fMP4; decoding that with `decodeAudioData` is not tried here. A browser that cannot decode it finds out within the first pieces and the aligner says "not available". Desktop Safari plays through hls.js when it can (it then takes the same path); WebKit may well not decode the pieces. Sticks with little memory will mostly end up on the voice detector alone (still an improvement over the file's times, less precise than with words).
- Whistle is English only, so a film with another spoken language gets voice-detector alignment only (no auto-pick, no words). It has no Romanian; it also knows Italian, German, French, Spanish, Dutch and Polish, which could be added once tried on real tracks. A stream that reports its sound as "und" counts as possibly English, so a film in another language makes a device fetch the recogniser and listen for a few minutes until nothing it hears fits any track.
- A film whose audio and video come muxed in the same pieces (`audiovideo`) on a slow device can lose stretches of sound: the pieces are megabytes, the queue holds 16 MB, and what is dropped from it is not sent again by hls.js. TS sources (audio pieces of ~100 KB) are not affected.
- Loading the recogniser is not cancelled when the film is changed during it; the next film starts its own. Nothing is kept between films (a series re-fetches the model from the browser's cache).
- The shift of the lines no solve has reached is one number for the whole file; a file that drifts, or is for another cut, is worse far from the stretches that were solved.
- The remembered manual Delay is kept across films and sits on top of the aligned lines.
- Subtitles that come in the HLS stream itself (hls.js draws them), plain `mp4`, native HLS.
- The kit's later options (TurboScribe words, a forced aligner): put them in the kit and bump `VERSION`; old entries are then not mixed with new ones.
